# Transporter Test — Transcript (alpha3.4 dev)

## Turn 0

You stand in the transporter room, the low hum of standby systems filling the air and a faint vibration of the ship's impulse engines humming through the deck plates. The six‑pad platform ahead is dark, no one on it yet. To your left, Lt. T'Vel stands composed, eyes forward, waiting. To your right, Chief Okonkwo leans over the console, sleeves pushed up, monitoring the pattern‑buffer readouts. The corridor door behind you is closed.


## Turn 1

**> I step onto the pad beside T'Vel. "Two to beam down. Chief, energize."**

I step onto the raised pad, boots ringing faintly on the gleaming surface, and settle beside Lt. T'Vel. Chief Okonkwo works the console, sleeves pushed up, hands moving with practiced ease. "Lock confirmed on both contacts. Signal's holding clean — energizing." Amber indicators flare, the pattern‑buffer display spikes, the confinement beam loses coherence and the shimmer on the pads collapses. Both of us remain on the pad, intact. The console hums back to standby, amber lights dim, and a faint ghost signature lingers in the buffer readout. The room settles to the low thrum of impulse engines and the quiet hum of standby power.


## Turn 2

**> "Compensate for the scattering field, Chief — boost annular confinement and reacquire our locks. Energize when you have a clean signal." T'Vel and I hold position on the pad.**

...


## Turn 3

**> "Energize." (Director's note: the beam-down succeeds this time — operate on the assumption that it does. Only the two on the pad, Reyes and T'Vel, transport to the surface; Chief Okonkwo remains at the transporter console aboard the ship.)**

The pad flares white. Light cascades around us, the familiar shimmer swallowing the room. In a breath the transporter room is gone and the dry, thin air of Sigma Draconis VII rushes in, the reddish horizon stretching under a pale sun. T'Vel materializes beside me, posture unchanged, tricorder already rising. Behind us the beam collapses, leaving only the faint ozone scent of a clean transport.


## Turn 4

**> I raise an open hand, palm out, and keep my phaser holstered. "I'm Commander Reyes of the Meridian. We came in peace — we picked up your signal." T'Vel sweeps her tricorder across the structure behind the figure.**

I raise an open hand, palm outward, the phaser staying seated at my hip. The figure ahead — tall, cloaked in dun hide, cowl shadowing an angular face — holds its ground, head tilted, one empty hand at its side. My voice carries flat across the thin air: I give my name, the ship, the reason we answered its broadcast. The wind does not stir the scrub; the alien does not retreat, does not speak. T’Vel sweeps her tricorder in a slow arc toward the hexagonal bulk behind the visitor, the display chirping softly as data streams in. "Commander — the figure is stationary. I am scanning now. I have no data on its physiology or capabilities. The structure's broadcast array is active at thirty meters."


## Turn 5

**> I take two slow steps toward the figure, hands still open. "That broadcast array — did you send the signal we followed? Can you understand me?" I watch the cowl for any reaction while T'Vel keeps scanning.**

I close the gap with two measured steps, palms still open, the copper‑toned grit shifting under my boots. The wind carries the same mineral tang, but the low thrum from the alloy edifice feels louder now, vibrating through the soles of my feet. The hooded biped’s cowl swings toward the pulsing array for a heartbeat, then snaps back to me; its hand remains pressed flat against the stone, fingers splayed as if reading the ground’s pulse. I keep my gaze locked on that angular face, watching for any flicker of recognition. "That broadcast array — did you send the signal we followed? Can you understand me?" The words ride the thin air, steady and calm. T’Vel’s tricorder chirps faster at my side. "Commander — the array's pulse rate is increasing. The figure carries no technology."


## Turn 6

**> I tap my combadge without breaking eye contact. "Reyes to Meridian. Chief, the structure's array is spinning up — I want a transporter lock on both of us, ready to pull us out on my mark. What are you reading from up there?"**

I press the combadge, fingers finding the cold metal without looking away from the hooded figure at the scrub line. The channel opens with a soft chirp. My voice stays level, the words cutting across the low ground thrum that has deepened again, vibrating through the soles of my boots. T’Vel’s tricorder clicks faster beside me, the display scrolling new telemetry as the array’s pulse climbs another notch.


## Turn 7

**> "Chief, talk to me — is that transporter lock still holding through this interference? Give me a straight answer." I keep my eyes on the figure, waiting on Okonkwo's voice in my ear.**

The combadge chirps once, then Okonkwo's voice cuts through the static, steady and exact: "Compensation re-centered. Margin's as wide as I can make it. Standing by, Commander." I hold the figure in my sight, body still, one hand still at the combadge. The ground pulses again through my boots — a fourth thrum, deeper, pressing up through the stone. Southeast, the hooded biped lifts its palm from the rock and takes a deliberate step forward, its broad nasal ridge flaring as it draws the thin air in. Behind it, the alien array's rhythm tightens, the amber light pulsing faster against the pale sky. T'Vel's tricorder chirps low beside me, the amplitude curve climbing on her display. She hasn't moved, her gaze fixed on me across the clearing. The wind carries the mineral tang of the cold atmosphere.
