# The Ashen Quest

Fifty beats through the real Sonder pipeline. Every model output is
authored by hand; every mechanism that fires is the engine's own.

Maelor is **dormant and off-screen** for almost the whole quest, and
so is Wren, who went east to break his workings before they set. The
player never watches either of them. What they do reaches Corin as
consequences that fired on a clock and as things other people saw.

Neither of them foils the other completely, and neither is narrated.

Lines in **bold** are what the engine did, not what was narrated.

---

### 1. I look into the well.

The water has gone the colour of ash and will not settle.

### 2. I ask Sera how long it has been like this.

Sera says three days, and that the Fenwater went first.

### 3. I ask who would do this.

Bryn says the name people are avoiding: Maelor, in the Ashen Keep.

### 26. I check the cistern.

It is fused shut. Nobody heard it happen.

### 27. I tell them we are going to the gate.

Sera says she will go ahead. Bryn says he will hold the town.

### 28. I let Sera go ahead.

Sera goes out through the culvert.

### 29. We move to the gate road.

The Citadel Gate is a slot in the rock, and it is cold.

### 30. I look for Sera.

No sign of her. The gate is watched by nobody Corin can see.

### 31. I check the gate for a way through.

There is a seam in the rock that was not there yesterday.

### 32. I put my hand on it.

Warm, and cracked across. Somebody got here first and did not finish.

### 33. I look for another way.

The culvert Sera named runs under the gate.

### 34. I take the culvert.

It is a tight, wet, and entirely unwatched way in.

### 35. I come up inside.

The Ashen Keep is warm, and it is not empty.

### 36. I look for him.

The hall is long and there is a figure at the end of it.

### 37. I say his name.

Maelor answers without turning around.

### 38. I ask him why the wells.

He says water is the only thing nobody can refuse to need.

### 39. I tell him the Vale will not kneel.

He says the Vale has been kneeling for eleven days.

### 40. I ask what he did with Sera.

He says he has not touched her, and Corin cannot tell if it is true.

### 41. I draw the sword.

It is a smith's apprentice's sword and it is not enough.

### 42. I go for the seam in the wall instead of for him.

Corin brings the hilt down on the warm seam. It rings.

### 43. I hit it until it breaks.

The seal fails, and every sealed thing in the Vale fails with it.

### 44. I look at him.

Maelor is not frightened. He is recalculating.

### 45. I ask him to stop.

He says he will not, and that he does not have to win quickly.

### 46. I wait for him to move first.

He moves first.

### 47. I take the blow and close the distance.

It costs Corin his shield arm and buys him three feet.

### 48. I finish it.

It is quick, and afterwards the hall is very quiet.

### 49. I go and find Sera.

Sera is at the gate, cold and furious and alive.

### 50. We come down through the market square.

The square is quieter than the morning they left it, and the grain stalls stand stripped bare.

### 51. We go home.

The Fenwater is running. It is the loudest thing in the Vale, under the villagers' talk of riders and empty grain stores.
