# Ten turns on the repaired engine, against ten on alpha 9.5

Run 2026-08-18, after ~300 audit findings were triaged and ~180 repaired
across 268 commits. The suite was green at every step of that work, and a
green suite never runs a model: it can tell you nothing changed shape, and it
cannot tell you a character stopped reasoning well. This is the instrument for
the second question.

Design in [`../design/AUDIT_REPAIR_PLAN.md`](../design/AUDIT_REPAIR_PLAN.md)
§"Phase 4". Both installs ran on their own database, seeded read-only from the
owner's provider config; `engine.db` was never opened for writing.

## What was compared, and what was not

Ten scripted player inputs, byte-identical on both sides, packed 2–3 features
per beat. **Prose was not compared.** Two runs of one model produce different
sentences from identical input, and here the two runs were not even on the
same models — so a prose diff would be noise wearing the clothes of evidence.
What was compared is what the engine DID.

**The models differ, at the owner's direction**, to keep two parallel runs off
one provider. 9.5 ran entirely on OpenRouter (mercury-2 default, grok-4.20 on
director/character/narrator); 9.6 kept the live config (Fireworks
deepseek-v4-flash on director/narrator, glm-5p2-fast on character). That buys
freedom from contention and costs attribution: a difference could be the
repairs or the models. Where it mattered, the report says which it can and
cannot separate.

## The result

**Both installs completed all ten turns. Zero commit errors, zero rollbacks,
no missing stages, no tracebacks on either side.** Both ran the identical
plan: 5 steps on turn 0, exactly 9 on every turn after.

| signal | 9.6 repaired | 9.5 control |
|---|---|---|
| turns committed | 10 | 10 |
| commit errors / rollbacks | 0 | 0 |
| turns missing narrator or commit | none | none |
| stored variants per step | 1 (no rerolls) | 1 (no rerolls) |
| memories written | 86 | 35 |
| lore entries | 5 | 9 |
| relationship_events | **27** | **0** |
| standing contacts at end | 2 | 0 |

## The one result the model swap does not explain

`relationship_events` went from nothing to 27, and the cause is separable
because the character role was switched mid-run:

| | character model | `relationship_updates` emitted |
|---|---|---|
| 9.5, all ten turns | grok-4.20 | **0** |
| 9.6, turns 0–7 | glm-5p2-fast | 40 |
| 9.6, turns 8–9 | grok-4.20 | **8** |

The same model, in the same role, emitted nothing across ten turns on 9.5 and
eight entries across two turns on 9.6. Something in the 268 commits made
characters produce stance updates where they had produced none. The stories
had diverged by turn 8, so these are not identical inputs — but zero across
ten turns against eight across two is well outside what that explains.

## Two defects the run found that the audit did not

**A card that killed every turn.** A persona authored `"pronouns":
"they/them"` — the way anyone writes it — passed `normalize_persona_data`
and then raised `AttributeError: 'str' object has no attribute 'values'` on
the NARRATOR step of turn 1, and of every turn after. `_deep_defaults` merged
by key and checked presence, never type, so a scalar written where a mapping
belongs did not degrade the sheet, it changed its SHAPE, and every reader
downstream had been written against a shape the normalizer no longer
guaranteed. Fixed in `78024c4`: a default now defines a shape, and a written
paradigm is read rather than discarded.

**A room named after the player.** 9.5's scene ended carrying a room `Wren`,
adjacent to the lobby. Neither engine invents it — checked, by running the
merge on both trees with the diff that preceded it — so the Director authored
it, most likely from seeing `packet: Wren` in `positions`. But it lands on a
real gap: `_refuse_name_collision` guards minting a CHARACTER whose name is
taken, and says why ("names are IDENTITY here … `scene.positions`, the active
cast, addressing, perception routing and every psychology write are keyed on
them"). The same collision the other way — a ROOM taking a person's name — is
unguarded.

The first rule tried for it was "a room id is never a position key", and the
live corpus refuted it in one query: chats 61 and 74 legitimately hold vehicle
interiors sharing an id with their entity. The rule has to be narrower — a
room id must not equal a PERSON's name. Recorded rather than shipped, because
a rule that has already been wrong once deserves the corpus check before the
second attempt.

## The manifest is only as sensitive as the Director is diligent

Both Directors under-populate `changes_asserted` relative to the channels they
write — 9.5 asserted 20 entries against 38 populated channels (0.53), 9.6
asserted 11 against 30 (0.37). Some of that is legitimate: one entry ("she
steps into the lift") properly produces `positions`, `stations` and `poses`.
But 9.6 turn 4 asserted ONE change while writing EIGHT channels including
`cast_changes` and `location`, and turn 8 asserted none while writing three.

This is not an engine defect — the state committed correctly, and the manifest
exists to catch the opposite failure, a change narrated and never encoded.
What it means is that **the engine's only instrument for detecting a lost
change is a ledger the Director fills in voluntarily.** 9.5 listed 20 things,
was caught omitting 4, and paid for 3 repair rounds: the safety net working.
9.6 listed 11 and was never caught, because there was less to catch it on.
Zero repairs reads like quality and is partly just a smaller net.

## Timing, which is a config finding and not an engine one

9.5 ran ~40s per turn; 9.6 ran 72–272s. Per-stage, from gaps between step
completions (crude, and applied equally to both):

| stage | 9.5 grok | 9.6 deepseek/glm |
|---|---|---|
| interaction_loop (the character step) | 11.0s | 122.7s |
| director_resolve | 17.8s | 27.6s |
| narrator | 3.6s | 6.2s |
| perception_outcome | ~0.2s | ~0.2s |
| stored bytes/sec | 1,755 | 689 |

**The character step is the long pole by an order of magnitude**, and switching
`character_major` to grok-4.20 mid-run cut it from 153.8s (turn 6) to 49.1s
(turn 8) on the same engine and the same story. `glm-5p2-fast` is not.

Two things worth keeping from that table. `perception_outcome` is 0.2s every
turn on both — the deterministic perception layer composing every observer's
view in a fifth of a second while the model stages take minutes, which is the
argument for it in one number. And the growth in 9.6's per-turn wall clock is
NOT context accumulating: stored output per turn plateaus (123k, 158k, 137k,
151k), it does not climb.

## What this does and does not license

It licenses saying that ~300 findings' worth of repair did not break the
ability to play: ten turns, both installs, no rollbacks, identical plan shape,
and the deterministic layers behaving identically.

It does not license a quality claim. Ten turns is ten turns, one scenario, one
pair of model configurations, and the two sides were not model-controlled. The
`relationship_events` result is the one finding that survives the confound,
because the character role was switched mid-run and the control held.
