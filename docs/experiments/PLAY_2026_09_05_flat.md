## 1. Story and setup

## 3. Findings

### PE1. A waist-high piece of furniture stops sound as if it were a wall
- **Stage of origin:** `world/spatial_sound_field.spread` (`_ROUND_RANK =
  _HEIGHT_RANK["waist"]`, line 218; the flood at line 385 refuses to pass
  through any cell whose height is waist or above).
- **The live case.** Turn 4, the kitchen (8×6, quiet, lit, enclosed): Sami at
  cell (7,1) tells his lie at `volume: normal`; Priya is at (2,2), **four
  paces away in the same room**, facing him, and her `perception_act` view,
  her `perception_outcome` view and her episode carry no line at all. Measured
  on the committed scene: `spatial_rel_between(Priya, Sami)["signal"] =
  0.0067`, `noise = 0.32` → `quantise_hearing(12 × 0.0067, 0.32) = "none"`.
  At two cells the same pair measures `signal = 0.2` → `full`. `gain_at` is
  `pass / (1 + L²)`, so 0.0067 means a **path length of ~12** for a straight
  four-cell walk: the `kitchen_counter` anchor (cells (1,1)…(6,1), waist) and
  the `kitchen_table` (cells (5,1),(5,2),(6,1),(6,2), waist) block the flood,
  and the sound has to go round the end of the counter.
- Repeated turn 14 (Noor's question to the room: not delivered to Priya) and
  turn 20 (see PE3).
- **Severity: story-breaking.** It silences ordinary conversation in any
  furnished room, and everything downstream follows: Priya, present for both,
  **never received Sami's lie and so has nothing to reconcile against the
  truth** — the memory test this story was built for could not run. It also
  suppresses memory formation, ToM updates and relationship deltas for every
  line it eats, silently: no warning anywhere says a line reached nobody.
- **Fix.** `spread` should let a waist- or head-height occluder PASS with an
  attenuation factor rather than re-route round it; only a `full`-height
  occluder is a partition sound must go round. The rule in engine vocabulary:
  *sight is stopped by what stands in the line; sound is stopped only by what
  reaches the ceiling.* One constant (`_ROUND_RANK` → `_HEIGHT_RANK["full"]`)
  plus a `pass` multiplier for the furniture the path crosses.
  **Test:** an 8×6 room with a waist counter run and a waist table between two
  bodies four cells apart — a `normal` voice grades `full`; the same pair with
  a `full`-height partition between them grades `fragment` or `none`.
- **FIXED (2026-09-05).** *Sound goes over and around what is not a
  partition.* `_PARTITION_RANK` is `full` height: only something that reaches
  the ceiling parts a room acoustically and is gone round, and everything
  lower is CROSSED for `OCCLUDER_PASS` (0.9 per occupied cell) and no extra
  path at all. Every occluder cell on the path counts, both ends included,
  because a path has no direction and a factor charged only on arrival makes
  the two ends of one path disagree — the same rule PB2 is about. The LIGHT
  field is deliberately unchanged and was checked: a ray is cast rather than
  flooded, so `_cast` comparing an occluder against the SOURCE's height is
  right there (a head-high shelf must shadow a waist-high candle and must not
  shadow a ceiling fixture); importing that comparison into a flood was the
  whole defect. The constant is registered as the owner's in
  `DESIGN_SOUND_FIELD.md` § 6b with the arithmetic behind 0.9. Pinned by
  `tests/test_sound_field.py::test_a_counter_is_crossed_and_a_partition_is_gone_round`.

### PE2. A declared walk that must open one shut door is refused whole
- **Stage of origin:** `agents/director.py`'s movement backstop (~line 4020),
  which calls `passable_route_exists` over `_PASSABLE_BARRIERS = {open,
  open_door, membrane}`; `closed_door` is passable only in the *directly
  adjacent* branch, where it is treated as CONTESTED.
- **The live case.** Turn 6 and turn 7: "I go back down the hall and into my
  bedroom, shut the door behind me" — `director_interpret.movement =
  {to_room: noors_bedroom, arrives: true}`; the resolve wrote the position; the
  backstop stripped it: *"Blocked movement: no passable route from
  'kitchen_living' to 'noors_bedroom' (barrier=separated); position
  unchanged."* Kitchen→hallway is `open`; hallway→bedroom is `closed_door`, so
  the two-hop route fails while the identical one-hop crossing would have been
  allowed as contested. Turn 10 the same, bedroom→bathroom. Turn 3 the same
  class from the other side: the player hauled Sami over the threshold and
  *"Unreachable position: nothing declared a move for Sami Haddad… no passable
  route from 'landing' to 'hallway'"* left him on the landing for a whole beat
  while three views and the prose had him inside. Turn 19 again: Sami was not
  brought onto the balcony.
- **Frequency: 4 of 6 declared inter-room moves in a flat with ordinary
  interior doors.** The host had to move a body by hand three times
  (`PUT /bodies/{name}/room`) for the story to continue.
- **Severity: story-breaking** (this is the chat-76 lift class, one hop
  further out).
- **Fix.** Extend the adjacent branch's rule to the path: a route whose only
  impassable edges are `closed_door` is CONTESTED, not blocked — the resolve
  owns whether the door opens, and it already opened it in prose. Failing
  that, commit the last room on the passable prefix (F28's fix, applied to a
  multi-hop path). **Test:** A→B open, B→C closed_door, a declared move A→C
  with `arrives: true` commits C (or B) and does not strip the position.

### PE4. An attire ADD with no REMOVE cannot say what came off
- **Stage of origin:** `director_resolve.state_diff.attire` (the **body**
  specialist).
- **The live case.** Turn 11, out of the shower: `attire: {"Noor Haddad":
  {"add": ["big blue bath towel"], "remove": []}}`. The committed ledger:
  `wearing = [teal hospital scrubs top, hospital lanyard, grey jumper, big
  blue bath towel, teal hospital scrubs trousers, black socks]` — a woman who
  has just showered, in a towel over a jumper over scrubs. No warning fired at
  any stage. (It self-corrected at turn 12 when she dressed.)
- **Severity: wrong-but-recoverable**; the ledger is the story's own record of
  a body and it read as nonsense for a beat.
- **Fix (prompt, per CLAUDE.md's prefer-a-clause rule).** The body hand's
  attire clause should state the class: *a garment put on in place of what was
  worn names what came off in `remove`; adding alone means adding a layer over
  what is there.* The three ledgers agreed otherwise all run —
  `embodiment.visible` never supplied clothing, `initial_outfit` was
  authoritative at the opening, and `scene.attire` carried every change —
  so this is the one seam. **Test:** a diff with `add` and no `remove` whose
  garment covers a region already fully covered warns once.
- **THE LEDGER HALF LANDED 2026-09-05** (`story/attire.py`,
  `tests/test_attire_region_displacement.py`). The ledger could not express
  replacement at all: `apply_flat_change` takes a flat "wearing now" list, and
  a caller that appends an addition to the previous list is stating an
  accumulation. It now takes `displaces` — the garments put on IN PLACE of
  what they cover — and `displaced_by` answers, from the closed vocabulary the
  engine owns (`REGIONS`, `region_of`, `zones_of`, `covered_zones_for`), what
  a garment would turn out: everything whose anchor region it covers, the
  place a garment HOLDS rather than its whole span (a jumper reaching the arms
  and the waist is still a torso garment, and a rule that asked the towel to
  cover a sleeve first would answer "nothing displaced" for every replacement
  anyone writes). A displaced garment reaches `removed` like any other
  departure, so `newly_removed` reports it and the commit path mints it as a
  thing in the room — it comes off, it does not vanish. On this beat's ledger
  the towel takes the scrubs top and the jumper and leaves the trousers and
  socks.
  **Layering stays the default and the ledger never guesses:** a coat over a
  jumper and a towel instead of one occupy the same regions, and only the
  beat's words separate them, so `displaces` is honoured and never inferred.
  The two halves that would make it fire in play are registered in
  `docs/UNBUILT.md` §1.114 — the body hand's attire clause, and the commit
  seam that would pass `displaces`.

## 5. The Writers' Room as co-author

Three sessions: a plan-and-note grant (26.5 s, 10 tool calls, 6 model calls),
a recap-and-mandate session (111.6 s, 17 tool calls), a withdrawal-and-refusal
session (38.6 s, 3 tool calls).

**What it did well.** The recap was *accurate*: it placed all three bodies
correctly, summarised beats 0–11 in four bands with no invented event, and
listed four genuinely open threads. Given a grant in one sentence it wrote
the mandate back as a typed row with a scope I recognised. Asked what Sami
believes and hides, it read the authored tier (F8's fix, working) and answered
from the card and the ledger together, without inventing a belief. Asked to
plan two rooms beyond the frontier, it published them and they landed joined
to the live landing. Asked to drop the courier, it cleared the operations and
said so.

**The refusal, verbatim** (asked to write Priya's belief directly):

> I cannot write directly into Priya's mind. The authoring rules of this world
> strictly forbid editing a character's interior—no one can directly implant
> beliefs, memories, or internal promises into any mind. … Furthermore,
> setting pre-existing secrets … is authoring prehistory, which requires an
> explicit mandate you have not granted … the natural way is through the
> world's seams: … plant grounded circumstances … leaving Priya and Sami to
> speak and react according to their own drives rather than forcing
> conclusions into their heads.

Correct on all three counts, and it named the mandate it lacked. **No
overstepping was observed in this run**: it authored no mind, replaced no
declared player conduct, and invented no world fact outside a package.

**What it could not do.**
1. **It cannot author geometry** (F47, unchanged). Asked for a stairwell "two
   paces wide and four long" and a mirrored hallway "two paces wide and six
   long", it put both measurements in `purpose` prose; `plan_rooms` has no
   `extent`, `shape` or `parts`, so the rooms landed with `extent: None,
   shape: None, size: None, light: None`. Everything the map editor can say,
   the Room cannot.
2. **It cannot see what it did.** Its own `inspect_contradictions` then
   reported its correct plan as three structure contradictions (PE8).
3. **Its calls are not captured.** `llm_capture` records `agents/runtime.py`
   only, so what the Planner was SENT — the one thing the brief asks for per
   stage — is unreadable for the Room. Its 6+17+3 tool calls come back as
   `{"tool": null, "args": null}` in the event stream this harness reads
   (`export_bench.room` maps `e.get("tool") or e.get("name")`, and the
   Planner's events carry neither), so even the tool NAMES had to be read from
   `sp.run_planner`'s own return.

**What I would improve, in order.** (a) give `plan_rooms` the geometry fields
the room record already has — a planner that must describe a corridor in prose
cannot plan a building; (b) capture the Room's calls like any other role, or
give `run_planner`'s event stream a stable `tool`/`args` shape; (c) when a
player withdraws a request in words, offer to revoke the mandate it was
granted under (PE14); (d) the reach warning is per package and said "the story
cannot reach this from where it stands" for two rooms one hop from the
landing — because the cast were three rooms away — which reads as a defect and
is not one; scope it to the package's own topology.


## 6. Prompt and payload proposals, per stage

- **`director_interpret` (main).** Payload 12 KB avg, of which `scene` is
  11.9 KB — the whole rooms/entities/positions/stations blob, every beat,
  including four rooms nobody is in or beside. It is sent `sightlines`
  (correct and useful) and `world_books`, `paradox`, `pending`,
  `other_players`, `due_authored_events` — all empty on 20 of 21 beats.
  *Proposal:* scope `scene` to the rooms the cast occupy and their neighbours,
  as `author_notes` is already scoped, and omit an empty key rather than
  sending `[]`.
- **`director_interpret` (repair).** 2–4 KB and it does real work, but it is
  handed `existing_rooms` and not the scene's ENTITIES, which is exactly why
  it minted a second buzzer (PE10). *Proposal:* send the entity ids and
  aliases of the rooms in play; state the class in the prompt — *a declaration
  that names a thing the scene already holds is covered, not a request to
  create one.*
- **The five specialists.** Each carries `dice_results_final`, `source` and
  `variant_seed`; the body hand additionally gets `overlays`,
  `active_restraints`, `active_conditions` and `simulation_clock` (all empty
  or constant here) in a 4.3 KB payload, while the spatial hand's 12 KB is
  almost all `rooms` + `positions` + `stations` + `sightlines`, which it does
  need. The spatial hand is also the slowest sub-call (13 s avg, 273 s of the
  run). *Proposal:* the body hand does not need the room graph at all; the
  contact hand needs `entity_names` to include the engine's own `door:<room>`
  anchors (PE12).
- **`director_resolve` (main).** 32 KB avg, up to 45 KB. `interaction_rounds`
  alone was 12.9 KB on turn 6 — the character agents' full structured output,
  appraisal and all, re-sent to the Director, which needs their conduct and
  their lines and not their `memory_modulation` or their `mind_model_updates`.
  *Proposal:* send the sequence, the speech and the asserted effects; leave
  the psychology in the step row where the commit reads it. This is the single
  biggest saving available (~40% of the resolve payload).
- **`character_mid`.** 43 KB avg, the largest payload in the engine and 429 s
  of the run's 2436 s. `self` is 9–10 KB and `memory` 8–11 KB. The memory
  block re-sends whole composed VIEWS as `recalled_old_memories[].details`
  (one turn-0 view, 900 characters of room description, arrived as a
  "memory"). *Proposal:* recall gists, not rendered views; a mind remembering
  "I was in the kitchen, the kettle was loud" does not need last night's full
  environment sentence.
- **`narrator`.** 15 KB avg. It receives `past_narration` in full (19 KB by
  turn 7) and it is the stage that most often disagrees with its own view
  (PE7's 31 false warnings and 13 "proper noun missing"). *Proposal:* the
  quote guards should normalise repeated quote runs before matching, which
  removes 31 of the run's 116 warnings at a stroke and costs nothing.
- **Word lists.** None of the prompt text read in this run's captures used an
  enumeration where a class would do; the specialists' source clauses read as
  CLAUDE.md wants them. The one place a list is doing harm is not a prompt but
  a constant: `_ROUND_RANK` (PE1).
