# Review record — 2026-11-01 codebase review

Status: EVIDENCE, written while the review ran. The durable record of the
2026-11-01 whole-codebase review: what was read, what was found, what was
split, what was fixed, and what is recommended. Companion to
`docs/design/SPLIT_COMMON.md`, `docs/design/SPLIT_COMPOSER.md` and
`docs/design/SPLIT_PERCEPTION.md`.

Every `file:line` cited by the audits is **as of commit 84f3de23** (the state
before the first split). The three splits moved most of `agents/common.py`,
`agents/composer.py` and `agents/perception.py` into siblings, so a line
number in those three files is a citation into the pre-split revision, not
an address in the tree.

## 1. Method

- **Backup first.** The parked layout-round work was committed (84f3de23) and
  the branch pushed; it had been 98 commits ahead of the remote.
- **Baseline before any change.** Full suite on `.venv`: 19,635 passed, 4
  xfailed, 0 failed, 281 s at `-n 6`. The offline real-pipeline driver
  (`tools/story_drive.py` on a scratch database) ran 23 turns with 0 errors in
  12 s; it was re-run after every split and is the between-changes engine
  smoke.
- **Reading.** 39 read-only audit lanes, one per subsystem, each given the
  same brief (what the project treats as a defect, the evidence standard, the
  registers it must not re-report) and a list of questions specific to its
  files. Every lane read its files in full and wrote a report; the reports are
  in this directory, unedited except for the scratch paths. Two
  information-gathering lanes digested the UNBUILT registers and measured the
  test suite (`tests_health_raw.json`).
- **Splitting.** One planner per large module produced a plan JSON; one tool
  (`tools/split_module.py`, landed by the tooling lane) applied it: a verbatim
  partition of every top-level statement, free-name analysis through
  `symtable` with annotations collected separately, a sibling DAG check, a
  facade with explicit re-exports of every former name, and a post-write
  re-import that compares the facade's namespace with the original's. The
  anatomy input came from `tools/module_anatomy.py`.
- **Fixing.** Seventeen fix lanes with disjoint file ownership, each writing a
  failing test before its fix and a log of what it fixed, skipped and
  requested; the language-runtime lane ran first because the others call the
  helpers it landed (`fold_key`, the corrected `name_boundary_pattern`,
  `split_sentences`).

## 3. Classes that recur across lanes

These are the shapes that appeared in five or more reports; each is a rule
the engine lacks rather than a defect in one file.

1. **English-only text assumptions in a language-pack engine.** `\b` word
   boundaries, `.split()` word counts, `[a-z0-9]` slugs and English word lists
   appear in every package. Measured consequences: a Japanese room name folds
   to the empty string (`normalize_room_id`), two kana-named owners' shed
   jackets collapse onto one object, a Japanese featured-resident history
   always fails its word-count gate, the disguise tripwire never fires, rumour
   degradation does nothing, a deaf card still hears, a Japanese question is
   never detected. The shared matcher `name_boundary_pattern` itself put no
   boundary at a kana edge. The fix is one set of script-aware helpers in
   `language_runtime` and the call sites routed through them.
2. **Substring matching where a boundary was meant.** Room names in goal
   text ("beginning" names the Inn), planned rooms ("the spinning mill" lands
   in the `inn`), beat-ending cues ("center" matches "enter": 52 of 153 live
   beat-ending acts matched inside a longer word), salience cues ("liar" in
   "familiar"), room-exposure words ("wood" in "wooden": 20 of 666 live rooms
   read falsely open), the entrance rank ("Great Hall" ranks as a hall).
3. **Two representations free to disagree.** The `world_entities`
   projection is skipped by the World Browser, `world_put`, the commit's own
   mints and the attire folds; `room_registry` is written without the scene by
   `plant_structure`; a body's whereabouts live in sixteen scene stores with
   writers that update fewer than all of them; the FTS mirror, the embedding
   model key, summaries and the rows they summarise each have one writer that
   forgets the other.
4. **Writes outside `transaction()`.** Plot clocks (`fire_due_clocks`),
   `publish_package`, mandates on four threads, artifact wording, background
   promotion, the charter's `save_registry`, World Browser read-modify-write,
   `planned_entities` marked `rendered` during preparation — each reproduced
   as a lost or doubled write.
5. **Module-level "last call" state read by the next caller.**
   `providers.last_reasoning` is not cleared between calls and is handed to
   the next mind in the interaction loop; the embedding coalescer serves queued
   callers on the leader's abort; the dressing caches outlive a deleted chat.
6. **Guards that fire on, or miss, valid output.** Facade monkeypatches that
   went inert and stayed green; `assert`-by-absence tests; a schema reachability
   test satisfied by a class name in a comment; the facade rule reading only
   absolute imports; the patch-target rule blind to string-form patches.

## 4. The splits

See the three plan notes. Lines before → after: `agents/common.py` 12,971 →
facade + 16 siblings (170-1,253 lines); `agents/composer.py` 7,691 → facade +
12 (315-1,279); `agents/perception.py` 9,323 → facade + 12 (357-1,470). Each
split was committed alone, with its repoints, and the full suite was green
after each (one pinned test and one doc line needed the new module names).
What the repoints taught: between a move and its repoints, 8 (common) and 23
(perception) tests failed and several more passed vacuously; ten of the
common repoints and all five module-source pins were invisible to
`project_check`, and only the splitter's repoint table found them.
