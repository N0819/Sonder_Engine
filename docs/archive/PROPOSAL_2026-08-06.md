## 1. The spine: one generator unlocks the two largest features

The two biggest unbuilt subsystems share step 1, and `UNBUILT.md` §2.7 says so
outright: *"gap-history plus delta-summary is the valuable 80% and is the same
generator as §2.8."*

**The gap generator** — one subject-agnostic answer to *"what changed about X
since turn N"*, where X is a character **or a room**. Nothing of it exists
`[read]`: no `gap_history`, no `delta_summary`, no function of that shape
anywhere in the tree.

### 1.0 The goal, stated first, because it decides the shape

The point is **the illusion of a living, changing world** — not a simulation of
one. That distinction sets the cost model and it is the thing to design against:

- **Away from the player: low resolution.** Coarse, deterministic, no model
  call. *The garrison changed shift. The market closed. She was in the east
  wing.* Enough that the world is not frozen; not enough to contradict anything,
  because it asserts almost nothing.
- **A major character acting in the background: medium resolution.** A sketch
  with enough specificity to matter when you meet its consequences — the plan
  advanced, the letter sent, the alliance made — but still short of on-screen
  detail.
- **Nothing offscreen gets full resolution.** Full resolution is what the
  on-screen pipeline is for.

**Resolution is not a character tier.** It is computed per tick from two axes:

```
resolution = f(distance from the player, importance of the character)
```

Neither alone is enough. A major antagonist three continents away does not need
medium resolution — you will not meet the consequences for fifty turns. A minor
character in the next room may warrant it, because you are about to walk in on
whatever they did. **Medium resolution does not mean medium NPC.**

This matters more than it looks, for three reasons:

1. **It is recomputed, not stored.** A character's importance is fairly stable;
   their distance is not. So resolution belongs in the tick decision, not as a
   field on the character. The villain sharpens as you approach their sphere and
   dims when you leave it, without anyone editing a setting.
2. **The world sharpens where you are looking**, which is exactly the illusion
   being bought, and it is bought by never paying for detail you cannot yet
   perceive. That is the architecture's *cost scales with dramatic density, not
   story length* applied to the offscreen half.
3. **It self-limits.** The count of characters who are both important *and*
   near is small in any given stretch of story, and it shrinks automatically
   whenever the player is somewhere quiet.

What this corrects in the current design: `offscreen_life` is a chat-level
ceiling `[read]`, so it can only make *everything* richer or *everything*
poorer. `BehaviorController` per character (§1.2 step 3) supplies the
**importance** axis and is necessary but not sufficient — the distance axis has
no representation at all today.

**Distance is not metres.** The useful measure is closer to *how many beats
until the player could meet this, or its consequences* — room adjacency, transit
edges, and whether a standing intention aims at where the player is. The place
graph already holds most of that.

### 1.0.1 Three methods, not two — and only the top one may create consequences

Resolution selects a **method**, and there are three rungs rather than two. The
middle one is the cheap majority case and I had collapsed it into the top:

| Rung | When it runs | What it does | Cost | May it change the world? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Background — lazy** | **only when something finally interacts with them** | a time-tick summary of what they did, generated retroactively and conditioned on the interaction that asked | **zero until contact** | no |
| **Deterministic** | on cadence | position, clock, scheduled events. *She was in the east wing.* | none | no |
| **Profile summary** | on cadence | a plausible account from their **profile** — no psychology run, no adjudication. *She spent the week in the archives, as she would.* | one cheap call | **no** |
| **Full agent** | on cadence | offscreen perception → declares from psychology → **the Director adjudicates** | 3–4 calls | yes |

**The bottom rung is the default, and it costs nothing at all.** A background
character is not ticked. Nothing about them is computed until another entity
acts with them — and at that moment you generate what they were doing for the
intervening turns, backwards.

Three things follow, and the first is the reason the gap generator is the spine
of this proposal rather than one component of it:

1. **The lazy rung *is* the gap generator.** "What did X do between turn N and
   now", produced at the moment of contact, is literally
   `gap_for(subject, since_turn=last_seen, until_turn=now)`. It is not a
   separate mechanism — it is the same function, called on demand instead of on
   a cadence. Build it once and the entire bottom of the ladder is free.
2. **Conditioning on the interaction is what makes it cheap *and* coherent.**
   You ask the barkeep about the barrow; the summary of their last fifty turns
   is generated knowing that is what was asked. Detail appears exactly where it
   is needed and nowhere else, which is a better outcome than pre-computing
   detail and hoping it was the right detail.
3. **It is genuinely `O(re-contact)`.** The design's own economics, achieved
   rather than approximated: a cast of two hundred that the player never meets
   costs nothing, forever.

The three cadenced rungs above it exist for one reason only — **a consequence
the player must be able to meet *before* meeting its author**. You cannot lose a
race that was never run. Everything else can be lazy.

**The line that keeps this safe: only the full-agent rung may create
consequences.** The three below it may *describe*; they may not *commit*.

A profile summary can say *"she spent the week in the archives"*. It must not
say *"she formed an alliance"* — that is a consequence, and no Director ever
adjudicated it. Without that line the cheap rung quietly becomes an unadjudicated
authoring channel, which is the same failure as a villain declaring victory
offscreen, arriving through the back door and harder to see.

Two ways to enforce it structurally rather than by prompt:

1. **The record shape differs by rung.** Only the full-agent rung emits
   `deltas`, standing-intention writes, or events with ids. The lower rungs emit
   `summary` text and nothing a downstream consumer treats as fact. A rung that
   *cannot* express a consequence cannot smuggle one.
2. **Novel specifics route through `background_claims.py`** regardless of rung —
   `record_claims`, `unratified_claims`, `settle_claims`, `CLAIM_TTL_TURNS = 8`
   `[read]`. A name or place a summary invents is a claim with a TTL, ratified on
   contact or expired. That machinery already exists for background presences and
   is the right home for this.

This also makes the economics much better than the two-rung version. Most
characters who deserve *any* attention deserve the middle rung, which is one
call and no Director involvement — so the expensive top rung is reserved for the
handful actually driving plot.

### 1.0.1a What the full-agent rung is: a reduced turn, not a sketch

For the top rung only. The distinction that makes it buildable rather than a
second engine: a major character offscreen runs **the same loop the on-screen
pipeline runs, with fewer inputs and a coarser clock**. Not a prose generator.

An ally gets a **summary of what is happening in the world around them** —
their offscreen perception, deliberately low-fidelity — and **responds out of
their psychology**: gathering allies, pressing a plan, mounting an escapade. A
villain does the same and spends it on stacking the deck. Then, and this is the
load-bearing part, **the Director adjudicates whether it worked.**

That last clause is what keeps the tier honest, and it is the architecture's own
personality-versus-ability cut applied offscreen: a character authors what it
would *attempt*; whether it *succeeds* is objective and belongs to the resolver.
Without it, a villain simply declares victory offscreen and arrives
unstoppable — the offscreen equivalent of a character being spookily prescient.

So the medium tier decomposes into four steps that already have on-screen
analogues:

| Offscreen step | On-screen analogue | Cost (off the turn path) |
|---|---|---|
| A world summary, coarse, for one character | perception pass | free — assembled from state |
| That character declares an attempt, from psychology | character step | one bounded call |
| The Director adjudicates success/failure | director resolve | one call, or seeded dice |
| Consequences commit as durable world state | commit | free |

**Three consequences for the build:**

1. **The gap generator is not the whole of it.** It answers *"what changed about
   X"*. This needs the inverse as well — *"what does X know about what changed"*
   — which is the same function pointed the other way, and is where the offscreen
   summary comes from. Build `gap_for` so it can serve both directions.
2. **Standing intentions are the substrate, and they exist.** The architecture's
   *"off-screen world state with standing intentions and triggers"* is what a
   villain's stacked deck actually is. `standing_intentions` is already a
   frame-scoped world key `[read]`. The plan advancing offscreen should write
   there, not into prose.
3. **Adjudicated outcomes must be seeded and logged**, for the same reason
   on-screen dice are: a reroll must re-derive the same offscreen history rather
   than quietly producing a second one. This is the architecture's *"seeded,
   logged, replayable"* clause, and it is the one place in the offscreen design
   where I would refuse to compromise.

**What this does to the ordering.** It makes the medium tier depend on the
Director seam, which is the most delicate part of the engine — §5.3 already
warns that the reaction/director boundary should be approached test-first and
small. So: build low resolution first and completely, ship it, and let the
medium tier follow once the gap generator and its reader are proven. Low
resolution alone already buys "the world is not frozen"; medium buys "the world
moved *against* you", which is the better feature and the riskier one.

### 1.0.2 Run it out of band, on a cadence — and the engine already has the pattern

The medium tier costs three or four model calls per acting character. On the
turn path that is unaffordable. Off the turn path it is nearly free, and the
objection disappears.

**The precedent is in the tree and should be copied rather than reinvented.**
`backdrops.py`'s out-of-band queue exists for exactly this reason, and its
header states the rule in words `[read]`:

> *Nothing may WAIT on an image... A caller asks for a backdrop, gets "ready" or
> "pending" straight back, and a worker thread produces it.*

It carries `_QUEUE_LOCK`, an `_IN_FLIGHT` map so two requests for the same work
produce one worker, a `_LAST_ERROR` map, a `status()` returning
`ready | pending | error | absent`, and a named worker thread. Every one of
those has an offscreen-tick equivalent. Reuse the shape.

**Parallel is free here for a structural reason, not a lucky one.** Offscreen
characters declare blind to each other — the same property that lets on-screen
character steps run concurrently. The architecture names it: *"simultaneous
blind declaration lets them run concurrently, so per-turn latency is roughly the
slowest single character's think-time, not the sum."* Offscreen it is better
still, because nothing is waiting on the result at all.

**Cadence: produce every N turns, deliver on contact.** These are two different
clocks and conflating them is the mistake to avoid.

- *Production* every ~3 turns bounds cost and gives the world a heartbeat.
- *Delivery* happens at re-contact, or when a consequence reaches the player —
  which may be twenty turns later or never. A tick nobody ever meets is not
  wasted; it is the world having been somewhere you did not look.

**Three hazards, each with a fix the codebase already demonstrates.**

1. **Staleness.** A tick reads turn N's world and lands at turn N+3. Stamp every
   tick with the `base_turn` it read, and validate on landing: if the world moved
   incompatibly, discard or re-run rather than commit. This is exactly §4.4's
   *"prepared commits touching shared canon should carry a base revision and
   reject or reprepare when the revision changed"* — the same guard, arriving
   from a different direction, which is an argument for building it once.

2. **Rollback.** If the player rolls back past a tick's `base_turn`, that tick is
   about a future that no longer happens and must be invalidated. This project
   has already paid for the general version: a checkpoint restore silently undid
   a completed embedding rebuild because the restore path put old vectors back
   verbatim, and the repair was to have the restore hand the work back to a
   reconciler. An offscreen tick needs the same treatment, and `base_turn` is
   what makes it decidable.

3. **Silent failure.** `backdrops.py` keeps `_LAST_ERROR` for a stated reason —
   *"out-of-band work that fails silently is worse than work that fails
   loudly."* A tick that dies must leave a row saying so. That is the same
   `basis: "unavailable"` rule from §1.2, and it is why it is in the record
   shape rather than bolted on.

**What this changes about the ordering.** Latency stops being the constraint on
the medium tier, so richness is bounded by cost and coherence rather than by
turn time. It does *not* move the tier earlier: it still depends on the gap
generator, a reader, and the Director seam. But it removes the reason to keep it
thin once those exist.

It unblocks, in dependency order:

| Feature | Blocked on | Register |
|---|---|---|
| Off-screen life that is *visible* | gap generator | §2.8 |
| Reactivation negotiation | gap generator | §2.7 |
| Interim filler on return | gap generator + `last_seen_clock` | §6.1 |
| Predictive staging | nothing — independent | §2.9 |

**Why it is the right first move.** Cost stays `O(re-contact)` rather than
`O(cast × turns)` — most of the cast never needs a tick, because the gap is
generated when you next meet them. That is the design's own economics and it is
what makes a large cast affordable.

### 1.0.3 Compliance: every rung is gated on locations, and on canon through claims

This is the property that decides whether the feature is an asset or a slow
corruption of the world, and there is already one row of evidence on which side
it lands unaided. The single `offscreen_log` record ever written places Picard
in *"a quiet office"* `[measured]` — a room the scene graph does not contain.
One tick, one invented location, and nothing downstream can reason about it.

Two different problems, needing two different mechanisms.

**Locations: a hard, deterministic gate at every rung.**

A generated summary may not name a place in prose. It must resolve to a
`room_uid` (or a registered entity interior) that already exists, and the check
is cheap because the place graph is authoritative and deterministic — no model
adjudicates this. Three outcomes, and no fourth:

- resolves to a known node → accept;
- resolves to nothing → **reject and regenerate once**, then fall back to the
  rung below (a deterministic *"she was elsewhere"* is worth more than a
  plausible lie);
- names somewhere the character could not have reached given the place graph
  and the elapsed clock → same treatment. Reachability is computable and this
  is the check that stops a character being in two regions in one week.

This gate belongs at **every** rung including the lazy one, and it belongs on
the write path rather than the read path. A stored invented room is a defect
that outlives the turn that made it.

**Canon: claims with a TTL, not a second validator.**

Novel specifics that are *not* locations — a name, an object, an event, an
alliance — go through the mechanism that already exists for exactly this:
`background_claims.py`, with `record_claims`, `unratified_claims`,
`settle_claims` and `CLAIM_TTL_TURNS = 8` `[read]`. An invented specific becomes
an unratified claim; contact ratifies it, or it expires.

And the architecture already describes the pre-commit shape for the on-screen
half — *"every new specific the narrator coins runs a fast neighborhood check;
collisions rejected, clean additions committed."* The offscreen path should use
**that** validator, not a second one. Two validators drift; this file has
several worked examples of what that costs.

**Provenance: offscreen output is its own tier, and it is provisional.**

This is where the feature meets §3.4's canon provenance tiers, and it is an
argument for building them sooner rather than later. An offscreen tick's output
is none of the existing dispositions — there was no Director (below the top
rung), it is not a player claim, and it is not a character belief. It is closest
to `inferred_mapping`: **provisional until corroborated.**

So the tiering is not decoration here. It is what lets an offscreen summary be
useful without being canon, and what lets the engine later tell the difference
between *"the world recorded this"* and *"a cheap rung guessed this while nobody
was looking."* Without it, every summary is indistinguishable from resolved fact
the moment it is stored — which is the same laundering §2C describes on the
promotion path, arriving from a new direction.

**One consequence for the ordering.** §3.4 moves up. It is listed under
hardening, but the offscreen feature needs at least the *provisional* tier to
exist before its output is stored anywhere durable. Building the feature first
and the tier afterwards means a migration over rows nobody can classify in
retrospect.

### 1.0.4 What the corpus says, and the hole where the mechanism should be

Three measurements against the live database, run after the design above was
written. They changed it.

**The top cause of a player paying to un-happen a turn is not contradiction and
not thinness. It is their declared action not landing as declared.** Twelve
branch pairs, ten with a divergent input, categorised by what the player changed
about *their own* text: **6 rewritten so the action lands** (hedging removed,
outcome written in, passivity replaced), 3 re-synced to something the world had
said, 1 physical premise corrected `[measured]`.

That is the architecture's own named central failure, observed: *"in a single
model the narrator is both eyes and hand — which is why such systems put words
in your mouth."* The starkest case has the player writing the *outcome* into
their own input, taking over the narrator's job.

**The loop closure.** The sharpest example is chat 56 turn 11 — the player typed
*"Which lever?!"* and the narrator answered **and grabbed the lever for them**.
That is the same turn carrying the only five stale steps in 1,727
`[measured]`: the player started a rerun, abandoned it after ord 3 — the splice
where the interaction loop says *right hand* and the narration says *left* — and
then branched, rewriting the input as *"You grab the lever."* The mechanism was
diagnosed this morning; the motive arrived twelve hours later from the other
end.

**Branching under-counts.** A rerun is the cheaper repair and sits beneath a
branch; a rerun that *succeeded* leaves no branch row at all. So the branch set
is the residue of failed repairs, not the population of dissatisfaction.
Variants-per-step is the tier beneath it with 1,727 turns as a denominator, and
it holds the player's input constant across attempts, which a branch does not.
Queued.

**Richness: reference density is supported, and my cheap fix for it is not.**
The miso reads rich and the footstep reads decorative, and the difference is
that one was referenced across three consecutive turns and the other once
`[measured]`. But the two conditions are otherwise **the same object**: same
in-world second, same lineage, both `active=1`, neither expired, empty state on
both, near-identical payload size `[measured]`. If riding the payload were the
driver, the footstep would have recurred too.

So *"make active conditions ride the payload every turn"* — which this document
carried as the cheap richness fix — **is not established and has been removed.**
It may be necessary; the control shows it is not sufficient.

The live candidate is a third thing neither of us proposed: **upstream
re-assertion.** The miso is not one condition persisting but three rows minted
in sequence — a craving, then a room-subjected meal prep, then a character-
subjected preparation — each minted *after* the prose referencing the previous
one `[measured]`. That is a feedback loop through the mapping stage, and if it
is what produces richness the fix is at neither site this document proposed.
The discriminator is queued: for each turn whose narrator prose references a
condition, whether that condition id appears in the same turn's upstream
variants.

**A device that already works and should be made deliberate.** *"Downstairs,
wood and paper settle with a distant creak, too muffled by the wall to carry any
words."* The firewall rendered as atmosphere — the world asserted to continue
offscreen with **no offscreen content generated at all**. Make it a payload
affordance rather than an accident.

**The identity defect, third instance, in the table richness depends on.**
Branching re-mints every condition id — the same TARDIS is `328f8a89…` in chat
38 and `63fa924d…` in 59 — and `kind` carries ~115 distinct strings over 329
rows, more than half occurring once, including *"minor self-inflicted bite
mark"* as a kind `[measured]`. If richness is reference density, reference
requires finding a thing by the same handle twice, and the longest-lived chats
are branches, which is exactly where the handle changes.

**Whose preferences these are.** Every number above comes from one corpus that
appears to be one player's stories. It is a measurement of what *this* player
rewinds. That belongs in the claim, not in a footnote to it.

### 1.2 Build order

**Step 1 — the gap generator.** New file: `gaps.py`.

One function, `gap_for(subject_kind, subject_id, since_turn, until_turn,
resolution)`, returning a structured record — never prose:

```
{ subject:    {kind: "character"|"room", id: <char_id|room_uid>},
  resolution: "low" | "medium",
  since_turn, until_turn,
  moves:      [{turn, from_room, to_room, basis}],
  events:     [{turn, event_id, summary}],
  deltas:     {…domain-scoped, e.g. relationship axes…},
  basis:      "deterministic" | "model" | "unavailable",
  producer, inputs, seed }
```

`resolution` is chosen from the subject's own tier, not passed by the caller's
mood — a caller that can pick the expensive tier is a caller that will. Low is
assembled from state the engine already has (positions, clock, scheduled
events) with no model call; medium adds one bounded call over that skeleton, so
the deterministic trail is what the prose has to stay true to rather than
something it invents around.

Three properties, each from a defect already on the record:

1. **`subject.id` is an id, not a display name.** `background_presences` is
   keyed by display name and that is the live Guinan defect; `offscreen_log`'s
   one row keys `actor` by name too `[measured]`. Both break on rename and
   cannot be joined to `chat_chars`.
2. **Room references are node ids**, never prose. The *"quiet office"* row is
   the argument.
3. **A gap that could not be produced returns `basis: "unavailable"` with a
   reason, never nothing.** Silence is how the abort path made a crash and a
   closed tab indistinguishable, and how `add_lore` ran without a model stamp
   for months.

Touches: `commit.py` (write path), `scene.py` (the ladder), `db.py` (world-KV
key registration only — no schema change).

**Step 2 — a reader, which is also the whole bottom rung.** The generator is
worthless until something surfaces it, and the cheapest useful consumer is
exactly the lazy rung of §1.0.1: when another entity acts with a background
character, call `gap_for` with `since_turn = last_seen` and inject the result
into the character payload that already carries `world_knowledge`.

That single step delivers the default behaviour for the entire cast at zero
standing cost. Everything cadenced is an optimisation on top of it, for the
minority of characters whose consequences must arrive before they do.

Touches `agents/character.py`, `agents/common.py`. Needs `last_seen` per
subject, which nothing records today `[read]` — that is the one new piece of
state the bottom rung requires.

**Step 3 — the two axes.** This is the step that delivers §1.0, and it is where
`BehaviorController` finally gets consumed — but only as the *importance* half.

*Importance* should follow something the engine already knows rather than a new
field a user must maintain: cast membership, and whether the character has a
sheet, memory and psychology, is already the engine's own line between a
background presence and a major character. A manual override belongs on top of
that default, not instead of it. Keep the chat ceiling as a maximum so no
running story gets louder without being asked.

*Distance* has no representation today and needs one. Not metres — beats-to-
contact: room adjacency and transit edges out of the place graph, plus whether
a standing intention points at where the player is. A first version can be
crude (same room / same region / elsewhere) and still capture most of the value,
because the decision it feeds has only two outcomes.

The resolution decision itself is a small pure function over those two, called
per tick. Keep it pure and it is trivially testable, which matters because it is
the thing that decides spend.

Touches `scene.py`, `commit.py`, `schemas.py`, plus the NPC dialogue panel.

**Step 3a — the profile-summary rung.** One call, no psychology run, no
adjudication, and structurally unable to emit a consequence (§1.0.1). This is
the rung most characters will actually use, so it is worth landing before the
full-agent one — it delivers most of the felt effect for a fraction of the cost
and touches none of the Director seam.

**Step 4 — make the low tier actually cheap.** The shipped `stochastic` rung
costs a model call; the design wants a seeded draw against standing intentions
with **none** (§2.8). This is the difference between low resolution being free
for the whole cast and being affordable for six of them, so it is what makes
§1.0's economics real rather than aspirational. Seeded and logged, so the same
gap re-derives identically on a reroll instead of quietly becoming a second
history.

**Step 5 — reactivation proposal.** Mapping proposes gap-history + delta-summary
to a returning character.

**Step 6 — negotiation.** Refusal budgets, identity-violation vs preference
tagging, stalemate-eats-canon. The hard, novel half; it trails deliberately.

**Precedent to reuse, not reinvent:** `background_claims.py` is already the
"commit invention as claims, not facts" mechanism this needs — `record_claims`,
`unratified_claims`, `settle_claims`, `CLAIM_TTL_TURNS = 8` `[read]`. A tick's
invented specifics should route through it rather than a second system. *Caveat:
I have read its signatures, not its bodies.*

### 1.3 Predictive staging (§2.9)

Independent of all the above and a pure latency win with no correctness
implication. Worth doing **after** the gap generator, because it is the item
whose absence nobody can see.

## 2. The structural item the register calls its largest

Separate from the feature work, and worth understanding before it gets more
expensive.

`UNBUILT.md` §4.2: *"Three of this file's largest items are one missing
structure."* Those three are §3.1 (prose matching as the concealment and
identity boundary), §4.2 (evidence-carrying perception), and §2.1 (citing the
present beat by its real id). All three want **stable signal / event identity**.

`grep signal_id` returns nothing repo-wide `[read]`.

**Why it matters now rather than later.** It is a retrofit-class item: every
turn played adds rows that lack the identity, so the cost of adding it grows
monotonically. That is the same shape as the lore embedding stamp landed this
week — `lore_entries` had no `embedding_model` column, and by the time anyone
asked, **1,031 of 1,418 entries** were carrying the crc32 fallback with no way
to tell which `[measured]`.

**Recommendation.** Do not build the full evidence graph. Land the *identity*
only: a stable `signal_id` minted where events and perceptions are already
created, carried on the objects that already flow, and stored where those are
already stored. Consumers can be converted one at a time afterwards. The
expensive, irreversible part is minting the id; the conversions are ordinary
work that can wait.

## 2A. Identity: the retrofit both halves arrived at independently

I reached "stable signal identity" from `UNBUILT.md` §3/§4; the assistant reached
**stable subject identity** from §1. They are the same family and the subject

**Five ledgers key beings by display name, and it is one defect, not five:**

## 2B. A third category, and a gate

My drift-versus-unbuilt split was incomplete. The register's own fire-rate data
forces a third class:

**BUILT AND NEVER FIRED** — the mechanism exists, is reachable, and has had zero
or near-zero occasions. `projects`: 0 of 14 banks. Disputes: 0 of 181 eligible

Importance revision: 9 of 6,480 (0.14%). Meanwhile aspect rankings, argued dead,
were top-16 on 39 of 40.

It needs a different fix from either of the others — **give it an occasion, then
measure**, not *improve it*. §1.23 Phase 2c is the worked example: the project

project. No prompt argues with a key that is never present.

**Proposed gate, and I would adopt it:** nothing gets built on top of a §1
mechanism until `tools/fire_rates.py` has reported that mechanism's rate against
a real denominator. This is the discipline that found three dead mechanisms in
this project already.

## 2C. The one item that writes a permanent falsehood

§1.8 — the autonomous promotion path builds a promoted character's opening
memories from the full `resolved_event` of every turn mentioning their name,
**including concealed acts, with no perception filter**, and writes them with

That is the omniscient record re-entering a later context, tagged as though

write path, durably, with a provenance stamp that launders it. It is blocked on

against), which is probably why it has survived looking like an importer bug.

**Immediately after the identity work.** It is the only item in the register

Each is small, each replaces an instruction with a structure:

## Open questions for you

1. **What promotes a character to medium resolution?** My default would be
   derived rather than declared — a character with a sheet, memory and
   psychology is already the engine's own line between background presence and
   major character, so it needs no new field anyone has to maintain. But
   "acting in the background" is partly a *story* judgement, not a structural
   one: an ordinary innkeeper who has become the plot deserves medium, and
   nothing in the schema knows that. A manual override on a derived default
   covers both, at the cost of one more dial.
2. **Does the Director adjudicate offscreen outcomes synchronously, or in
   batch at re-contact?** Per beat is truer and costs a call per acting
   character per beat; batching at re-contact is `O(re-contact)` like the rest
   of the design but means the villain's plan resolves in one lump when you
   walk in, which is harder to make feel gradual. My inclination is batch, with
   the seeded draw taken per beat so the *history* is granular even though the
   adjudication is not.
3. **Should low resolution ever be wrong?** A deterministic tick asserts almost
   nothing, which is what makes it safe — but "almost nothing" still eventually
   contradicts something on screen. The `background_claims.py` machinery already
   handles this shape: register the invention as an unratified claim with a
   TTL, let contact ratify or expire it. My inclination is to route every gap's
   novel specifics through it from day one rather than retrofit later.
4. **`signal_id` now or after the feature work?** Nothing is broken today
   without it, and it is the only item that gets strictly worse with delay. If
   the answer is "after", it is worth writing down *when* — a retrofit with no
   date is a retrofit that does not happen.
5. **Does anything read the gap besides the character payload?** The Director
   arguably should — a consequence you meet before meeting its author is the
   whole point of §2.8 — but that is a second consumer with its own leak
   surface, and I would rather land one reader and measure than two and guess.
