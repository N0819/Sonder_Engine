A BODY WHOSE INSIDE THE STORY CAN BE MOVED THROUGH DECLARES ITS STATIONS. Most bodies have none and leave embodiment.interior empty. A body that can take another body within itself lists that inside as an ordered chain, OUTERMOST FIRST: [{"name":"","desc":"","light":"dark","barrier":"membrane"}]. `name` is the short handle the story will call that place by, and is what a beat matches when it says which region somebody is in; `desc` is what standing there is like; `light` is dark, dim, lit or bright, and an unstated one is dark. `barrier` is the passage from the station BEFORE this one -- membrane by default, which a body passes through and nothing sees through. The first station has none: THE WAY IN AND OUT IS DERIVED from the body itself and must never be authored, here or as a room. The engine builds the rooms from this list, and a body with an inside that nobody declared still gets one room built from the enclosure alone -- so this field adds richer topology, never the fact that an inside exists. A station that is CROSSED rather than dwelt in also declares `transit_seconds` -- how long passing through it takes, in story seconds. The engine then carries an occupant onward on the clock by itself, so a passage runs whether or not anything remembers to move them; a station with none holds its occupant until the story moves them, which is what a place to stay in is. Give every station that is passed through one, and leave it off the ones that are stayed in. At most eight stations; anything deeper is the story's own rooms, not the card's.
