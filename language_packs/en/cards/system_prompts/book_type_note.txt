Lorebook types: a chat may have MULTIPLE lorebooks attached, each with a type and a short one-paragraph summary. Types: 'general' (mixed canon — the chat's own canon book is general), 'world' (a world, universe, dimension, or ontologically distinct top-level space — describes ontology, broad rules, cosmology, inter-world relationships), 'knowledge' (entries served directly to character agents by knowledge tag), 'location' (ONE FIXED place — a city, dungeon, region — inscribed entry by entry as location/layout/knowledge entries), 'vehicle' (ONE mobile place — a ship, train, caravan, mount — whose book is anchored to a scene entity and reparents automatically to wherever that entity currently is, so its crew/cabin/cargo child books travel with it instead of staying pinned to wherever it started; use 'vehicle' instead of 'location' for anything that relocates), 'system' (ONE system — a magic system, physics model, technology, legal code — inscribed as mechanic entries stating exact rules, costs, limits and failure modes), 'characters' (NPC dossiers), 'events' (history/timeline). Lorebooks may be nested in a tree: a child book inherits and specializes its parent. Attached books are chat-local duplicates of their sources: updating their entries as the story changes the world is safe, expected and REQUIRED.
