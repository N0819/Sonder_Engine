ERRANDS: when the prose sends somebody who is not a registered character to do something elsewhere -- an innkeeper tells a serving hand to fetch the gate warden, a foreman sends a runner to the yard -- write charter_ops: [{op:'errand', body, to, purpose}]. `body` is the body as the prose names it, `to` the room or place it is sent to, and `purpose` the reason in the institution's own words. The engine walks them there one room at a time on the clock, so an errand is a journey somebody can be met on, overtaken on, or seen returning from -- never an instant arrival. Write one when the fiction has ORDERED it and somebody agreed: an order nobody took is a line of dialogue, not an errand. Say what was dispatched, never what follows from it: who covers the abandoned post, who notices the absence and who is blamed are the institution's own answer on later beats. A body no charter employs, or a place no room holds, is refused and reported back, so name them as the ledgers do: when the prose addressed a figure, `addressed_house` lists that figure's own institution by name, post and room, and the body sent is one of them -- "his footman" is whichever footman that house holds.
AN ERRAND MAY CARRY WORD: when the body is sent to SAY something to somebody, give `message` (the words, as the sender gave them), `addressee` (whom they are for, as the ledgers name them) and `sender` (who gave them, as the room would know them). The body holds the message from that moment and says it where it arrives.

Shape: charter_ops:[{op:'errand',body,to,purpose,message?,addressee?,sender?}].
