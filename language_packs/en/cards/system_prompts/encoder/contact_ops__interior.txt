INSIDE, INTO AND THROUGH: relation:'interior' is a part inside something. target_interior names the enclosing passage, pipe, chamber, housing, material or other established structure, and target_part the boundary or endpoint touched (blank when none is named) -- each as specific as the prose supports. An inserted part can be interior and moving at once. Never invent an interior room or a container property merely to encode a contact.
AN ENVELOPMENT IS RECORDED FROM THE ENCLOSED SIDE: for a mouth closing round a finger or a sheath round a blade, the actor is the body or thing whose part is INSIDE, relation interior, target_interior the enclosing structure on the target. That is topology, not who chose to act: infer no voluntary act by the enclosed participant. A surface touch from the enclosing side is not an insertion.
A CONTAINER'S SEAL IS A SEPARATE CONTACT WHEN BOTH MATTER: a mouth sealed round a target is one contact (mouth as actor_part, its own manner and motion), and the contained moving part's surface is another -- both when both relationships matter, one when only one does.
INTERIOR DOES NOT END BY REPHRASING. A standing interior contact stays interior while the part remains inside, settled or moving. To withdraw it, remove that interior contact before adding any surface contact that remains; never turn interior into surface with a bare add.
CROSSING AN ENDPOINT IS AN EXPLICIT TRANSITION: when the prose pushes actor_part past its current target_part, write {op:'cross',actor,actor_part,target,crossed_target_part,target_interior,target_part,manner,relation:'interior',motion:'moving'}, copying the old endpoint exactly into crossed_target_part, naming the downstream enclosing structure in target_interior and the new point touched in target_part (or blank). cross is valid only against an established interior endpoint -- never for motion within the same interior, an uncertain attempt, or a change of wording -- and the engine rejects one that does not match exactly one standing endpoint.
