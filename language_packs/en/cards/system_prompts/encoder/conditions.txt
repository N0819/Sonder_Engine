CONDITIONS: a persistent condition the prose creates -- a wound, a fire, poison, a spell, flooding, contamination -- goes in conditions with condition_id, subject_id, kind, severity, started_at_seconds and state. Where it has a natural endpoint on the simulation clock (a flare burning out, a door on an auto-close timer, a drug wearing off), also set expires_at_seconds (absolute clock seconds), and the engine ends it when the clock passes that moment.

A CONDITION THAT ACTS WHILE IT LASTS -- anything that keeps changing something for as long as it stands (burning, draining, flooding, freezing, healing, working on a body) -- declares tick_interval_seconds, its cadence in simulation seconds, AND a `tick` object saying what one tick does: `vitals` maps a vital name (air, stamina, nourishment, injury) to the signed change each tick applies, and `percept` is one short clause the world keeps announcing while it runs. The engine fires the ticks on the clock, however many came due in the beat; the vital change applies only where the story tracks vitals, and the percept always runs. A cadence with no tick changes nothing, so declare one only where something is genuinely happening over time. THE INTERVAL IS A REAL NUMBER OF SECONDS, GREATER THAN ZERO. Zero does not mean constant or instant -- it is a condition that never acts, and the commonest way this table fails: accepted, correct-looking, and the fire it describes burns nobody for the rest of the story. For something that never stops, give the coarsest interval that still reads true -- how often a person would notice it change.

HOW A CONDITION ENDS: by the clock (expires_at_seconds), or by an act -- re-emit the SAME condition_id with active:false at the event that ends it (a new id opens a second condition instead of closing the first). One that declares neither stands for the rest of the story, so give it an end whenever the fiction has one. active_conditions in the payload lists every standing condition with the condition_id an ending must re-emit, its age, and whether anything will ever close it: re-assert what still holds, and end what has ended.

Three kinds of condition have rules of their own, shipped when the prose was judged to need them: conditions__awareness (a mind going under or coming back), conditions__restraint (a body kept from moving itself, and its release) and conditions__form (a body that changes what it is, or hides what it looks like). When the prose has one of those and its part is not below, ask for the part in missing_tools.

Shape: conditions:{condition_id:[{condition_id,subject_id,kind,severity,started_at_seconds,tick_interval_seconds?,expires_at_seconds?,state,active?}]} -- the value under each id is a LIST, because one subject can carry several readings of the same condition.
