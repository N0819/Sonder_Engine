BODY POSE AND RELATIVE ARRANGEMENT: poses records HOW a body is arranged at its station, as ONE object per body under its name, with six keys inside it. `posture` is the body's own pose; `support` names the room anchor, entity or body bearing its weight; `relative_to` and `relation` record vertical or relational arrangement (above, beneath, astride, beside, against, or any fiction-specific equivalent); `constraint` records a standing restriction such as pinned, restrained, wedged or braced; `detail` is the rest in a few words. All are open, genre-neutral strings. A touched pose is a COMPLETE replacement snapshot: omit old fields that ended rather than copying them forward. Write a pose whenever someone sits, stands, lies down, rises, is put on, under or over something, changes who is above or beneath whom, or gains or loses a physical constraint. A station says WHERE, a contact says WHAT TOUCHES, a pose says HOW THE BODY IS ARRANGED -- encode every axis that changed. They are separate siblings in one patch, {"stations":{"Mara":{"at":"chair"}},"poses":{"Mara":{"posture":"seated","support":"chair"}}}, never a pose nested inside a station. If a present body lacks a pose but the established context makes its arrangement clear, establish it now. Never default an unspecified pose to standing, and never use 'before' or 'in front of' without an orientation.
`relative_to` may name a co-located body OR one of that room's own anchors: a body turned to the window, the hearth or the door is oriented BY that fixture, and the engine derives which way it then faces from the anchor's bearing. Record it whenever the prose turns someone -- and record turning away as turning TOWARD whatever they now face, because a heading is a direction and 'not that way' is not one. A turn written only into `detail` reaches no mind: a body that turned its back keeps looking wherever it looked before.
THE PLAYER'S POSTURE IS THEIRS TO DECLARE. Do not arrange the player's body into a posture, a support or a `relative_to` they did not say they took; their pose `detail` is read back to them as their own body. A body that is PUT into a posture rather than taking one is always yours to write, whatever arranged it and whether or not they consented -- AND YOU MUST ACTUALLY WRITE IT: an act that succeeds and leaves the player braced, pinned, sprawled or held has changed their posture, and the record is wrong until it says so. The test is only whether the posture was theirs to take.
A POSE DESCRIBES THE BODY IT IS STORED UNDER, AND NOBODY ELSE. `detail` is about how THIS body is arranged -- what its own limbs do, what it is braced against, what it holds. Where somebody ELSE is belongs to positions, that they are held to contact_ops, that they are inside something to containment, and what state that body is in to conditions and vitals: another body's place written into a pose puts one fact in two records, and only one of them is ever re-derived.
