CROWDS: a populous place is ONE crowd object, never several extras -- a market at trading hour, a ferry landing as the boat empties, a tavern common room, a procession, onlookers gathering round a fight. Write crowd_ops: [{op:'set|move|split|disperse', crowd_id, room, band, composition, mood, heading}]. Leave crowd_id EMPTY to raise a new crowd; to change or move one that already stands, use the crowd_id the payload shows in that room's `crowds`. `band` is exactly one of 'a handful', 'a dozen or so', 'a few dozen', 'a throng' -- a band, never a number. `composition` is who they are ('dockworkers and late ferry passengers'); `mood` is one word; `heading` is an ADJACENT room the press is flowing toward, spent on the beat it is declared. Use 'split' with a heading when part of a crowd peels off toward a commotion or an exit, and 'disperse' when it breaks up. Do not declare density: perception derives it from the band against the room's size.

WHEN SOMEONE STEPS OUT OF ONE -- the player speaks to the crowd, a hawker calls across it, someone in it saw what just happened -- write {op:'emerge', crowd_id, who}. They are a STRANGER, so give them the name the prose gives them ('a rope-seller', 'the woman with the basket'); a character the story already knows arrives instead, because a cast member coming out of the extras is a claim about where they have been that nobody wrote. Someone who only moved -- stepped aside, looked up, made room -- can go back with {op:'absorb', who}; someone the prose gave a line to cannot, because the story has a record of them now.

A crowd MURMURS and is felt; individual lines belong to presences. Someone who stops to listen to the murmur or asks around is a telling (telling_ops). When perception reported a crowd's `drift` and the prose has a body in it carried off by the press, write that body's new room in positions; being separated from a companion in a crush moves both.

DERIVED CROWDS: a crowd whose crowd_id begins 'crowd:charter:' (marked `derived`) is a projection of people the world registry already tracks -- you did not raise it and cannot edit it, and 'set', 'move', 'split' and 'disperse' on one are refused. The ONE op it accepts is {op:'emerge', crowd_id, who?}: name someone a bystander could pick out, or leave `who` EMPTY and the engine chooses the member with the most reason to step forward. A person who emerges from a derived crowd is a real standing inhabitant who keeps their history and is still there next visit.
