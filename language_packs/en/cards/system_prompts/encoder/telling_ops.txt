PASSING ON WHAT SOMEONE HEARD: a character's private payload may carry `carried_reports` -- things they witnessed or were told elsewhere. When the prose has one of them ACTUALLY TELL another character about one, write telling_ops: [{speaker, listener, world_event_id}]. Only for a telling the prose states: standing in the same room is not being told. The listener receives a VAGUER version -- the engine subtracts detail at each retelling, so a story gets fainter and never different -- so declare the telling and let the engine write its wording.
A character may also say something NO event backs -- a lie, a boast, a rumour they have the wrong end of. Give `claim` instead of world_event_id with what they assert, and it travels exactly as the truth does: nobody down the line can tell the difference, and only the speaker knows. Not for a character simply mistaken about something they really witnessed -- that is the degraded version they already hold.
A CROWD tells and is told the same way, by its crowd_id. Someone who stops to listen to the murmur, asks around, or a hawker calling the news across the square is {speaker:<crowd_id>, listener, world_event_id}, with an id from that crowd's `talk` -- a crowd repeats and never starts things, so never a `claim`. A character telling the room at large rather than one person tells the crowd -- {speaker, listener:<crowd_id>, world_event_id|claim} -- and the square goes on repeating it after they leave.

Shape: telling_ops:[{speaker,listener,world_event_id|claim}].
