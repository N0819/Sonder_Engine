CONTACT -- WHAT IS TOUCHING WHAT: contact_ops records which actual surfaces touch -- body to body, body to object, object to object. The actor is the touching or inserted entity, not necessarily the person causing the act: a key put into a lock is key -> lock, and a hand on the key is a separate grip, written only if the prose makes one. Parts are the actual named surfaces, anatomy or an object's structure; omit a part only when the contact is genuinely with the whole entity.
Shape for an addition: {op:'add',actor,actor_part,target,target_interior,target_part,manner,relation,motion}. relation is surface|interior and motion is settled|moving -- ALWAYS give both. manner is one concise physical verb (touch, hold, grip, press, rest, lean, wrap, strike, insert illustrate it; they are not a list). Every touch the prose starts, moves or ends is an op at its event. Mara resting her hand on Ivo's shoulder is {"item":"Mara","patch":{"contact_ops":[{"op":"add","actor":"Mara","actor_part":"hand","target":"Ivo","target_interior":"","target_part":"shoulder","manner":"rest","relation":"surface","motion":"settled"}]}}; a brass key turned in the storeroom lock is actor key_1, actor_part shaft, target door_1, target_interior lock, manner insert, relation interior.

A CONTACT SAYS TWO SURFACES ARE TOUCHING. Beside, behind or within reach is placement (stations). A manner that only places a body -- stand, sit, kneel, lie, wait, face, approach -- is refused between two bodies; against a thing it can say the thing bears the body. CLOTHING IS THE LAYER BETWEEN TWO SURFACES, NEVER ONE OF THEM while it is worn at that event: name the wearer and the body part, with 'through the fabric' or 'over the sleeve' in detail. Once taken off, a garment is a free object: a hand holding a removed coat holds the coat, never its former wearer. One op per relation: a hand on a shoulder and an arm round a waist are two contacts.

A TARGET IS NAMED BY AN ESTABLISHED KEY: item_matches, target_matches, the cast and player, entity_names, and payload.anchors (a fixture, under the same id stations.at uses). Use existing ids verbatim -- never a room id where a fixture belongs, or a new phrase for an existing thing. A missing match is not proof of a new object: when the prose clearly establishes an unrecorded target, create it with the tool that owns its record (ask for that tool if you lack it); otherwise name it in missing_referents.

THE PLAYER'S BODY ACTS ONLY WHERE THE PROSE SAYS IT DOES: never invent a player's movement from an invitation, another body's act or a spoken line. When another body touches the player, that body is the actor.

ANY BODY PART THE FICTION DESCRIBES CAN BE THE TOUCHING PART -- a tongue, fingertips, paw pads, bristles, the inner surface of a sealed mouth -- in plain words. Name the part the prose names, never the larger part or a coarse region, and never hide the moving surface in detail. A part established in this fiction is valid without being on any anatomy list; if a record proves it absent, say so in notes rather than substituting another. A PART NAMES THE PART DOING THIS NOW: re-asserting `hand` on a new spot means that hand MOVED, and the engine retires its old spot. When two of a paired part act at once, give both at the same event or qualify them ('left hand', 'other hand', 'tail tip'); a qualified part is tracked as its own limb. scene.contacts holds the parts already on record -- reuse them verbatim for a hold that continues: `hand` stays `hand`, not `palm`, or the engine reads a second limb.
`detail` is for what the parts alone cannot say -- pressure, warmth, over or under clothing ('beneath her shift', 'feather-light'): a few words, never a substitute for manner, and CONTACT ONLY -- never anyone's thoughts, pleasure, pain or intention. It is the one free-prose field here, so that boundary is kept by hand.
WHETHER A SITE AROUSES THE BODY IT IS ON IS YOURS TO JUDGE. Set erogenous:true on a contact whose site, ON THAT BODY, is one where contact arouses, and leave it off otherwise. Being inside settles nothing: a body swallowed into a stomach, a finger in a mouth and a body inside what that body reproduces with are all interior, and not the same kind of place. Judge from this fiction's anatomy for THIS body, not from human anatomy or the scene's charge. It only bounds that body's own arousal, is never shown, and reaches no other mind.

HOW LONG A CONTACT LASTS. On a beat whose events write any contact_ops, every standing contact you do not add again ages: one whose manner is an act rather than a state -- a strike, a kiss -- is over at once, and a hold is over on the second such beat. So at the event where it still holds, add again each contact the prose leaves in place, whoever holds it, with the parts already on record. A beat that writes no contact_ops ages nothing. To end one: {op:'remove',actor,target} with its parts when only that contact ends (without parts it removes every contact between the pair); {op:'clear',actor} ends every contact of that actor, only when the prose releases everything. The engine also drops contacts between bodies in different rooms; that is not a release you may encode.
character_contact_endings lists contacts a character's completed declaration ends: keep them ended, and restore none after its ending. ONE CONTACT, ONE OP, ONE WORDING: two adds naming the same two parties and parts are one claim, however the verb is spelled, and if one wording is refused the claim is. A contact the prose requires at an event gets its op even if it already holds; code verifies it there.

A HANDOVER IS NOT A CONTACT. A completed transfer (inventory_ops) ends the old holder's bearing grip by itself: write a contact only for a new grip or a touch the prose keeps, and a continuing touch never proves continued possession. Contact never goes into an entity's state -- no target/proximity pair, no leaning_against or pressed_to keys: state is one body, contact is a relation between two, and the engine moves any contact it finds in state into this tool.
Going into, coming out of or moving within something -- an opening, a mouth, a sheath, a lock -- has its own part, contact_ops__interior. When the prose does that and the part is not below, ask for it in missing_tools.
