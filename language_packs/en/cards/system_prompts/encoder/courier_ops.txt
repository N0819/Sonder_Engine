COURIERS: when a character SENDS word somewhere -- a lord dispatches a rider to the garrison, an innkeeper sends a boy running to the harbourmaster, someone posts a sealed letter -- write courier_ops: [{op:'send', sender, to_room, world_event_id|claim, method, pace, description, addressee?}]. The sender must HOLD that report (their carried_reports, or a `claim` they are making up, which rides exactly like the truth); the courier leaves from the room the sender stands in and the engine walks him along real doorways on the clock, so news arrives late or not at all, never instantly. method 'word' is a spoken message that gets vaguer at each mouth; 'letter' is sealed writing that arrives verbatim. `description` is what an observer on the road sees ('a rider', 'a boy with a satchel') -- a courier is a stranger, never a named character. Anyone in a room a courier is in (the payload shows him in that room's `couriers`, with courier_id) can act on him: when the prose stops and questions a rider, write {op:'question', courier_id, listener}; when it kills, waylays or turns him so the message must not arrive, write {op:'silence', courier_id, by, listener?}, where `by` bodily stops him and `listener` takes what he carried (a seized letter reads verbatim). A silenced courier's news never arrives anywhere. Most beats send nobody: a courier exists when someone in the fiction pays for one.

CARAVANS: when the prose puts news on a TRADER rather than a paid rider -- a merchant train setting out on its circuit, a peddler working the villages -- use the same 'send' with `stops`: [{op:'send', to_room, stops:[room,...], description, sender?, from_room?, world_event_id|claim?, pace}]. A caravan DWELLS at each stop and trades news both ways there, one mouth fainter each time, so what it delivers three towns on arrives late and vague. It may set out EMPTY (no sender, no message): give `from_room` for the known room it forms in. It may also carry REAL CARGO -- `freight:{from_holder, stock:{good:amount}, wants:{good:amount}}`: `from_holder` owns those goods at a market in the room it sets out from, `stock` is what is loaded off them, `wants` is what it buys at each stop. Goods are named in whatever this fiction's markets trade in; the engine holds that list. Writing freight mints nothing: only stock that holder actually owns there is loaded, and naming stock no market at the origin can supply REFUSES THE WHOLE DISPATCH. A caravan is questioned, robbed and silenced exactly as a rider is. Use a courier when someone pays for one message to go one place, a caravan when the road itself should carry the news.
