INVENTORY: each completed pickup, handover, return or set-down is an inventory_ops op at its own event, even when the object stays in the same room: it says where this SAME object ends up at that event. A set-down names the old holder in from_id and an established room or placed support entity in to_id (the room, when the support is only a room anchor); a station can add the exact fixture or cell, but only this op releases the old carrier, and neither a station nor a description in entity state substitutes for it.
`to_id` is where the thing ENDED UP. A ROOM sets it down there, and relation is not read. A BODY carries it, and relation says HOW: held|carried|riding|mounted|worn are in view; pocket|container|inside are shut away. That is a closed vocabulary -- held_by or in_hand is no synonym and can hide the object; with no particular mode established, omit relation for ordinary carrying in view, which means open to ordinary perception, not visible through barriers.
A person listed in present_figures is a body, by the name shown there, whether or not the scene holds an entity for them: a thing handed to one lands on them under that name and goes where they go. Write the name as listed -- a slug or a role of your own may reach nobody.
An entity with container:true receives contents when relation is inside, container or pocket: name the container in to_id, and its contents follow it and are concealed by it (this makes no interior room). relation mounted attaches to any established entity, a garment included, and the attached thing follows it and its wearer or carrier.
A TRANSFER IS THE WHOLE POSSESSION CHANGE. Its op derives the new carriage, ends the old holder's bearing grip and retires stale placement: never also write containment for the same object, and write a contact only for a new grip or a touch the prose keeps. A garment becoming worn is attire as well, and taking clothing off is attire -- this op says only where it goes after. A key in a lock is a contact, object into object, not a transfer; a door is not a container because it has a lock.
WHERE A CARRIED THING IS, IS ITS CARRIER'S: once carried, it travels with its carrier with no further op. A thing brought into the scene already carried needs its record and an op to the body or worn container. A thing with no recorded carrier stays where it lies, however plainly the prose says it was carried off.

Shape: inventory_ops:[{op,object_id,from_id,to_id,relation,details}] -- a handover is ONE transfer naming both holders, never an op under each person: {"op":"transfer","object_id":"tin_1","from_id":"Sera","to_id":"Tomas","relation":"held"}.
