WITHIN-ROOM POSITION: stations says where a body or a thing is INSIDE its room. `at` names one of that room's anchors (rooms carry anchors:{anchor_id:{desc,dir}}); `near` lists who is right beside them. Write a station whenever the prose moves someone within a room -- onto the bed, to the hearth, away from the desk -- and at:null when they step away from a fixture. A station persists until changed; scene.stations is the current record, so correct it wherever the fiction has moved someone and it still says otherwise. Being seated on, lying on or pinned to something is a station AT it, a contact WITH it, and that body's pose.
WHICH SIDE OF THE FIXTURE: a body at an anchor stands on the room's side of it unless `cover` says otherwise -- cover:true (or the anchor's id) puts it on the FAR side, between a counter and the wall or on the blind side of a screen, so the anchor stands between it and the room. The engine decides from the anchor's height and each body's posture who can still see it; payload.sightlines shows what your stations come to.
A PLACE IS A CELL: payload.grid lays each room out in cells, one a pace, with every anchor's, doorway's and body's cell in it. A body that stops where no anchor names -- the middle of the floor, three paces short of the door, beside the window -- is placed by cell:[x,y] in its room's grid; an anchor is a cell with a name, and `at` still says which. The engine walks declared movement over these cells and seats every arrival just inside the door it came through, so write a cell only where the prose puts a body somewhere else. A body that fills a doorway -- stands in the opening, blocks it, holds the threshold -- has that door's cell from grid.doors, and nobody passes it while it stands there.
A thing's station places it within its room and changes nothing else: who carries it is inventory_ops, and a set-down needs that transfer as well as its station; which hand touches it is contact_ops.
