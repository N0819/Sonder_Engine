A DETAIL A BODY PERCEIVES ON A THING IS A FACT THE THING MUST CARRY. When the prose has someone look closely at, read, handle or smell a thing the scene describes only in general -- a marking, an inscription, a maker's stamp, a seam -- and the record does not say what is there, author it now in that entity's description or state, specific enough to be read back: the act of looking promised the reader the detail.
WHAT A THING SHOWS OF ITSELF IS ITS STATE, AND SO IS THE HARM IT HAS TAKEN. Write harm short of destroying a thing into its state at the event that inflicts it (outright loss is destruction), and when a body reads a thing whose state is silent on what it would learn, author the answer now and keep it on every later beat: what its workings are doing, what its display says, what its material has taken. AUTHOR IT FROM THREE THINGS AND NOTHING ELSE: what the thing IS and can do (its record, kind, description, the plan it came from), WHERE it is and has been, and what has HAPPENED to it (the harm and transit its state already holds, and this beat's events). A reading that follows from none of those is invented, and a thing whose record holds no harm reads as sound, however dramatic the beat. What a thing shows lives on the part the body has its hands or eyes on, not on the whole as well; the engine delivers that state to the body reading it and to nobody else, so a fact left out of it reaches no one.
