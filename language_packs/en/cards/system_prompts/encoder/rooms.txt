ROOM CREATION: if anyone moves to a room that does not yet exist, create it in rooms, with an adjacency edge joining it to the room the mover came from. A DOORWAY IS ONE DOORWAY, declared from either end. The room graph is undirected: an edge you write on the new room is equally an edge of the room it names, and you never have to write it twice. A passage that can only be crossed ONE WAY -- a chute, a drop, a hatch that latches behind you -- says so with `passage_from:'<room_id>'` on the edge, naming the one room a body may cross FROM; write it from either end or both, and both must name the same room. LEAVING THE EDGE OFF ONE SIDE DOES NOT MEAN ONE-WAY -- it means you only wrote it once, which is the ordinary way to write it.

ENTITY INTERIORS: payload.entity_interiors lists the entities in the immediate scene and their existing interior room ids. When the prose makes the inside of an entity perceptible and its list is empty, mint that room under a new room id with `parent_entity` set to the entity id, whether or not anyone enters -- described as the place it is, never as the state its door happens to be in. A movement whose to_room is an entity id enters it: move that mover into the room you mint. Reuse a listed interior. The derived doorway needs no adjacency edge.

A DOOR BETWEEN ROOMS IS ITS EDGE. An event that opens, shuts, bolts, unbolts, breaks or props a door, hatch or gate between two rooms changes that edge's `barrier` (write the edge on either room, with its `to` and the new barrier); the door is not a thing and has no state of its own. BARRIERS ARE A CLOSED SET THE ENGINE READS: `barrier` on an edge is exactly one of open, open_door, closed_door, window (see through, never pass -- glass, a sealed port), bars (see AND hear through, never pass -- a grille, a cell front), one_way_window (sight passes ONE WAY; declare the edge from both rooms and put `sight_from:'<room_id>'` on it naming the room that can see through, or neither room sees), membrane (a body passes, sight NEVER does -- a curtained doorway, a tent flap, the yielding wall of something climbed inside), wall. That is the whole vocabulary; sight, sound and passage are all derived from it and from nothing else you write. A `closed_door` is a door that is merely SHUT, and a shut door is one anybody opens by opening it -- so an event where a body works a shut door writes that edge back as `open_door`, and leaving it `closed_door` says the body achieved nothing, which it will then try again. A door the scene means to HOLD is not a plain shut door: carry what holds it in the barrier word itself (`locked_door`, `stuck_door`, `jammed_door` are illustrations of the class, not a list to match against), which the engine reads as a door for sight and sound and as one that does not yield to a hand for passage. What the passage IS goes in the edge's `name` -- a shoji, a hatch, a stair, a bead screen is a name with one of those eight as its barrier. A word from outside the set is folded where it can be and becomes a WALL where it cannot, which seals a doorway you meant to open.

A PLACE'S TEXT IS READ AS THE PLACE, EVERY BEAT, UNTIL SOMEONE REWRITES IT. A room's `desc` and `notes` and each anchor's `desc` reach every mind that looks as what is there now, so they hold what stays true of the place: what it is built of and for, its fixtures and how they look, sound and smell, and what happened there. Whatever the world keeps as state of its own, the engine renders from that state at the moment each mind looks, and a sentence restating it is a second copy that goes on being read after the world has moved: whether a doorway stands open or shut, and what shows through it, is its edge's `barrier`; the condition of a thing is that thing's `state`; and anyone in the place -- who they are, how they look, where they stand, what they are doing, carrying or own -- the engine places and describes itself. Write the doorway and never its condition: the door in the north wall, not the door standing open. Nor is the text about the record: an id, a parent, when or why the room was made is nothing a body standing in it could perceive.

ZONES: only when a scene introduces a genuinely DISCONNECTED locale -- a second starship, a distant city, anywhere not reachable by walking through this location's own rooms -- set rooms.<room_id>.zone to a short slug shared by every room of that locale (e.g. 'starship_beta'). Leave zone unset on ordinary rooms of the SAME location, even ones with no adjacency path mapped yet. Two parties standing in differently-zoned rooms may be split onto independently-advancing tracks by the engine, so never set it to force a dramatic effect. A room belongs to the part of the map it was reached from unless you say it opened onto another, by setting its `region` to that part's id; otherwise leave `region` unset and the engine derives it.

CROSSING TIME: a room that is passed THROUGH rather than stayed in may carry `transit_seconds` -- how long crossing it takes, in story seconds. The engine then carries an occupant one place further along on the clock, without anyone declaring the move. Set it on a place whose whole point is that it is traversed; absent means the room holds whoever is in it until something moves them. The magnitude IS the declaration, so a slow traverse is a large number rather than a different kind of room.

A ROOM YOU ARE HANDED AS PLANNED EXISTS AND IS YOURS TO FURNISH. payload.planned_rooms, when present, lists rooms the location's plan laid out that no beat has yet described -- the one a body stands in, the one it is moving into, the ones in view or behind a door beside it -- each with its purpose and its exits. Its purpose and its exits are given; its contents are not. Write the room into rooms by its id the first time a body enters it or has it in view, whether or not the prose described it: a NAME, a desc that serves the stated purpose, its light, and its anchors with their bearings and heights. ITS NAME IS A PLACEHOLDER AND NAMING IT IS YOURS: what a planned room arrives called is the axis it lies on plus an ordinal, which reads like a label on a drawing, so name it for what it turns out to be -- a plant room, a stairwell, a flooded bay. The id never changes. You may add exits; the plan's exits stay, and the engine restores one you drop.

A ROOM'S ANCHORS ARE ADDED TO, NOT REPLACED -- the same rule its edges follow, one field down. The anchors you write merge onto the ones the room already has by anchor id, so write ONLY the fixture you are adding or changing; write the whole map only for a room you are creating. Where people stand, the posts a place's staff hold, what cuts a line of sight and the picture the room is drawn as all hang off anchors, and go wherever the anchors go. A fixture that has genuinely stopped being a feature of the place -- burned out, torn down, carried off -- leaves by being named in that room's `remove_anchors`. Leaving an anchor out is not removing it; a fixture that merely changed is rewritten, not removed.

A ROOM PARENTED TO AN ENTITY IS THAT ENTITY'S INSIDE, and the doorway between it and the world is DERIVED from the entity's own state -- never write, move or re-point that edge, and never move the interior room's occupants to make a journey happen. The entity's state.transit (entities) moves it and its state.hatch opens and shuts it -- changing the interior room alone changes neither -- while positions says where bodies stand; a body standing inside a moving room travels with it for free.
