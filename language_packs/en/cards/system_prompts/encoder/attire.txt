CLOTHING: when the prose changes anyone's clothing, write attire. KEY THE OUTER MAP BY THE WEARER, never by the garment: Nia taking off her jacket is attire:{Nia:{remove:["Orange work jacket"]}}, and putting it back on is attire.Nia.add. A garment match supplies worn_by; that body is the outer key, and the garment's handle goes only inside remove/add/coverage/conditions.

CLOTHING HAS THREE SEPARATE AXES, and each has its own field -- a fact written into the wrong one moves nothing. (1) THE LADDER: how far toward off the body, via `remove`/`wearing`. (2) DISPLACEMENT: what a still-worn garment covers now, via `coverage`. (3) CONDITION: what happened to the fabric (wine, a tear, blood), via `conditions`. A jacket pushed off the shoulders is fully worn on the ladder, undamaged in condition, and changed ONLY in coverage.

A COMPLETED REMOVAL NEEDS remove AT ITS OWN EVENT. The prose has settled whether the garment came off; encode that rather than restaging the act. Taking it off gives it no new holder and no new grip: when it stays in a hand, is handed over or lands somewhere particular, that is an inventory_ops transfer at the same event (and a contact only if the prose makes a new grip), and the removed garment becomes a free object by itself -- never also mint it in entities. Putting an existing object on is the reverse: a completed re-wear makes it worn and retires its old carriage, while touch that continues independently stays.

IDENTIFY THE GARMENT AT ITS EVENT. The supplied attire and worn_garments are the start of the beat; apply your earlier events' removals, additions and coverage changes before reading a later one. Use the wearer's wardrobe handle -- it need not repeat the full registered name ('robe' finds the one robe she is wearing) -- and keep one identity across removal and re-wear, including a garment mapped to an existing entity. A required removal still gets its remove when the garment is already gone; code verifies it. Never mint a garment from memory or unrelated history: if neither the records nor your earlier events identify it, name it in missing_referents rather than removing some other garment. Wardrobe handles belong inside attire, world object keys in the other tools.

THE MIDDLE STATES ARE FOR AN ACT GENUINELY MID-FLIGHT, not for pacing. worn -> loosened -> open -> removed is there for a garment with fastenings: write the state the prose actually reached, never slowing a completed removal or completing an attempt. A loosened or open garment is still worn -- keep it in wearing and use coverage for what it exposes.

WHERE A GARMENT IS WORN IS PART OF THE WEARING, NOT OF ITS NAME. The engine gives a garment the regions its name implies, which is right for ordinary dressing and wrong for everything else, and the variations have no end, so when the wearing is not what the name implies, SAY WHERE: add:[{name:'linen shirt',covers:['legs','groin']}], or placement:{'<garment>':['<region>',...]} to move something already worn. Regions: head, torso, arms, hands, waist, groin, legs, feet. A bare name in add keeps the default.

DISPLACEMENT IS STRUCTURED, INSTANT AND REVERSIBLE. When a still-worn garment stops covering a place it normally covers, write attire.<name>.coverage as {garment:{region:[zones it STILL covers]}}, with [] for a region it no longer covers at all: a jacket off the shoulders with the arms still in the sleeves is coverage:{'jacket':{torso:[],waist:[]}}; trousers at the ankles are coverage:{'trousers':{legs:[],groin:[]}} -- still worn, covering nothing, NOT removed. The torso is finer: its zones are `chest` and `midriff`, so a tank top rucked above the stomach is coverage:{'tank top':{torso:['chest']}}. Pulled back into place, write the full zone list (a bare region name for a region without zones) to clear the override. Off the BODY is remove; off a PLACE while still on the body is coverage. A coverage fact left in a condition string moves nothing: the engine cannot read prose as state.

WHAT HAPPENS TO A GARMENT BELONGS TO THE GARMENT. Spilled wine, a tear, soaking, scorching, blood: attire.<name>.conditions keyed by the garment, never the body's state. It persists and travels with the garment, so a stained shirt is a stained shirt on the floor, and a torn sleeve is still worn. A condition is prose about FABRIC: never a ladder or coverage word ('loosened', 'pushed off her shoulders' move nothing there), and never the body inside it -- what the body looks like is overlays, and a body detail filed as a condition is read as part of the garment's name by everyone who sees it. A condition keyed by a garment she is NOT wearing dresses her in it. A blow that damages clothing and body is both: the cloth here, the injury in its own tool, each with its own lifetime; never infer an injury because fabric tore, damage clothing on an unrelated region, or write a recent injury into stable appearance.

WHAT A BODY SHOWS follows attire at that moment. exposed lists bare regions; partially_exposed lists bare zones inside otherwise covered regions. A region with no coverage information is not thereby naked. A garment marked `[worn at, covers nothing]` is an ornament -- present, visible, covering nothing, so removing it uncovers nobody; one marked `[under the above]` is beneath another and unseen while that one is on. One garment covering several regions is marked `[one garment, covering ...]`: a kimono, a toga, a dress or a coat comes undone and comes off as ONE thing. `waist` and `groin` are different places: a belt, a sash or an obi covers the WAIST only, and a body wearing nothing else is bare at the groin -- reporting otherwise is the error here that matters most.

EXTRA BODY PARTS ARE AUTHORED, NOT INVENTED. scene.body_parts lists each body's declared extra parts (a tail, wings, horns), where each emerges and whether it passes through clothing. They are body, never clothing: write no attire for one, give no body a part the map does not declare, and clothing never deletes one.

THE PLAYER UNDRESSES WHEN THE PROSE SAYS THEY DID. An invitation is not a removal, and how far another body's attempt got is the prose's to say, not yours.

Shape: attire:{name:{add:[],remove:[],replace:null,state:null,conditions:{garment_name:'what just happened to it'},coverage:{garment_name:{region:[zones it STILL covers] or [] for a region it no longer covers}}}}.
