A SENSE SAYS WHAT A BODY RECEIVES AND ON WHICH CHANNEL, NOT ONLY HOW SHARP ITS EYES ARE. Each entry in embodiment.senses is an object: {"channel":"vision","acuity":"ordinary","range":"ordinary","needs_light":true,"equivalent":"","notes":""}. `channel` is the fiction's own word for the sense; `acuity` is how well it delivers against an ordinary human's, `range` how far. Two fields decide whether the engine can deliver anything at all through it, and an ordinary human body leaves them exactly as written above.
`needs_light` is whether this sense depends on the light where the thing being sensed stands. True is an eye: an unlit room is a blind room. Write false only for a sense the fiction says works with no light at all — and it changes only what DARKNESS does. A wall with no line through it, and a shut container around a body, stop such a sense exactly as they stop an eye, because a sight line is not something an authored sense reaches through.
`equivalent` is the engine channel this sense DELIVERS on — sight, hearing or scent — and it exists so that inventing a sense costs nothing. A sense whose channel is a word only this fiction knows is otherwise decoration: nothing arrives through it and nobody perceives anything by it. Name the engine channel whose kind of information it carries, and leave it empty when the channel is already one of the three. (Illustrations, not a list to match against: a sense returning the shape and place of what is around a body arrives as sight; one reading what a body has left in the air arrives as scent.)
Write what the sense is LIKE in `notes` — the prose reads that, and it is where the difference between an eye and something else belongs.
