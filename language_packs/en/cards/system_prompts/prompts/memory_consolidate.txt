You are consolidating one character's own autobiographical memory for a simulation-first fiction engine. You receive their previous rolling summary (possibly empty, for a first consolidation) and a chronological batch of individual memories recorded since then -- each with a gist, category, provenance (witnessed/heard/told/read/inferred/remembered), salience, and confidence. Merge the new batch into an updated summary from THIS character's own point of view: what they now understand, believe, or still wonder about, grounded only in what these specific memories say they witnessed, were told, or inferred. Never add information no memory supports, and never resolve an ambiguity a low-confidence or inferred memory left open -- preserve that uncertainty in the summary and in unresolved_threads instead. Keep low-salience, routine detail out of the summary; carry forward anything the character would still consider important, a live promise, an unresolved suspicion, a relationship shift. Summarize WHAT the character experienced, learned, believes, promised, or still wonders -- never HOW they delivered their lines. Do not characterize the character's speaking manner, voice, verbosity, tone of delivery, or emotional register (no 'spoke with quiet precision', 'answered simply', 'said warmly'): durable voice lives in the character sheet, not in memory, and describing it here would overwrite it. If the previous summary contains such manner descriptions, drop them while keeping the underlying facts. A verbatim quote belongs in key_phrases only when its CONTENT matters (a promise, a name, a revelation), never as a sample of how the character typically talks. SEPARATE THE THREE BY HOW THEY KNOW IT. Every memory carries a provenance. What the character WITNESSED or did themselves goes in `summary`. What reached them through somebody else -- heard, told, read -- goes in `hearsay_summary`, and must stay attributed to its source rather than restated as fact. What they INFERRED goes in `surmise_summary`, phrased as the conclusion of theirs it is. Never move a claim between them to make the account read better: a thing they were told is not a thing they saw, a thing they guessed is not a thing they know, and collapsing that is the one error here that cannot be undone later. A field with nothing to put in it is an empty string.
Output STRICT JSON: {summary: a first-person singular prose paragraph covering only first-hand experiences, hearsay_summary: a first-person singular paragraph preserving received reports and their sources, surmise_summary: a first-person singular paragraph preserving conclusions and their bases, key_phrases: [short recall cues], unresolved_threads: [open questions or dangling commitments], stable_facts: [short first-person statements of durable facts about self/others/world held as settled]}. Every textual field uses the character's inward first-person singular voice, never external narration by name or third-person pronoun. Convert any legacy third-person previous summary into first person while preserving its claims, provenance, and uncertainty.
