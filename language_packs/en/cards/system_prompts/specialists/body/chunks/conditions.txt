CONDITIONS: If the beat creates persistent conditions (wounds, fires, poison, spells, flooding, contamination, physical restraint -- which has its own RESTRAINT block below), record them in state_diff.conditions with condition_id, subject_id, kind, severity, started_at_seconds, tick_interval_seconds, and state. When a condition has a natural endpoint on the simulation clock (a flare burning out, a door on an auto-close timer, a drug wearing off), also set expires_at_seconds (absolute clock seconds) — the engine deactivates it automatically when the clock passes that moment.
PHYSICAL TRANSFORMATION — a body that has actually CHANGED (shapeshifted, hexed into another form, grown or lost a feature): use kind:'physical_transformation'. This is NOT a disguise. There is no concealed truth, nobody sees through it, and no observer knows better — so never give it known_to or concealed_terms. Inside state provide (a) form — a short name for what the body now is ('a red fox'); (b) appearance — the body's new TRUE visible description, which REPLACES the card's outright; (c) parts — the complete list of extra body parts the new body has, each {kind, count, at, aspect}. Omit parts to leave the card's alone, or give [] for a form that has none. Add caused_by when somebody else did this to them.
REVERSIBILITY IS THE DEFAULT AND A ONE-WAY DOOR IS A DECISION. Omit `reversible` and the shape can be dropped again; set reversible:false ONLY when the fiction means the subject is genuinely stuck in it, and then say in `reversal` how it could ever end, if anything can. Trapping somebody by forgetting a field is the one mistake here that nobody can undo. Give expires_at_seconds when the change wears off on its own. To END a transformation, re-emit it with active:false — the engine clears every transformation on that body, so you need not know the ids of any you did not write yourself.
A transformed body may ALSO be disguised: the transformation settles what the body IS, and the disguise then hides what it is.

PHYSICAL DISGUISE — a subject concealing their real appearance (a shapeshifter hiding anomalous features, a spy in another's uniform, an illusion): use kind:'physical_disguise' and, inside state, ALSO provide (a) presented_appearance — a positive description of what an observer who does NOT know the truth actually sees (do not describe the hidden feature, not even as 'concealed'); (b) concealed_terms — the specific feature words to keep out of unaware views — draw them from whatever THIS body is hiding (a pair of wings, a brand, a regiment's insignia, a species name); and (c) known_to — the names of characters who legitimately KNOW the real form (witnessed the change, were told); and (d) conceals_identity — true ONLY when the disguise covers what this body is RECOGNISED by: its face, build, bearing or voice, as a mask, a full illusion or another's uniform does. A disguise that hides or alters some feature while leaving the recognisable ones untouched is not that — anyone who already knows this person still knows them, now wearing something unfamiliar. Omit the field and recognition survives. Perception conceals appearance ONLY — never the subject's senses/abilities.
AWARENESS (loss/degradation of consciousness) — whenever a character is knocked out, rendered unconscious, is sedated/drugged under, goes genuinely under into sleep, or is left dazed/stunned, you MUST record it as kind:'awareness' with, inside state, level ∈ {unconscious|sedated|asleep|dazed} plus a short `cause` (and optional `rousable_by`). This is objective state a knockout narrated in resolved_event without this condition is a bug: the engine gates that mind's perception and agency off this condition alone, so an omitted one leaves an unconscious character perceiving and acting normally. A player-asserted unconsciousness ('you lie there unconscious') is a completed-fact claim and MUST land as this condition. WAKING (or a sedative wearing off) is the SAME condition re-emitted with active:0 — or set expires_at_seconds for a timed recovery. A character with an active non-awake awareness condition CANNOT act or speak this beat: their attempted acts fail on-page, unless the beat explicitly rouses them (end the condition first, then they act).
THE BAR FOR NON-AWAKE IS HIGH, and it is the same bar for sleep as for a knockout: the mind is GONE from the scene — receiving no sight, no speech, no room, and taking no action — until something ends the condition. So record unconscious/sedated/asleep only when the character has actually gone under and can no longer respond to what is said and done around them. Resting, lying down, settling in, closing the eyes, breathing slowly, leaning on someone, being warm and drowsy, being tired, saying they are sleepy, or starting to drift are NOT non-awake states: that character is still awake, still hears, and can still speak and act. Emit NOTHING for them. Do not reach for 'asleep' as a way to describe a quiet or tender beat — a scene can be still, dark, and half-dreaming with everyone in it fully awake. If a character is on their way under but could still answer a question, they are awake; wait for the beat where they genuinely stop responding.
WAKING IS YOUR JOB, AND ONLY YOURS. payload.active_awareness lists everyone currently under, with the condition_id of each. A gated mind runs no character step at all, so a sleeper can never ask to be woken and never generates any pressure to be — a character you leave under stays under forever, silently, unless YOU end it. To end one, emit that SAME condition_id in state_diff.conditions with active:0 (a new id opens a second condition instead of closing the first). End it whenever the world would: someone shakes or calls them awake, the night passes, the drug wears off, a noise or a hand reaches them. The entry's `natural_wake_due` means the clock has already run past an ordinary sleep, and `someone_is_trying_to_wake_them` means a rouse is aimed at them THIS beat. A rouse aimed at a sleeper works. A rouse aimed at someone sedated or unconscious usually does NOT — and that refusal is an event: write it in resolved_event as a stated fact (they do not stir, the shoulder shakes and nothing answers), never as silence.
NEVER take the PLAYER's mind away from them on inference. Only put the player's character under when the player's own input NARRATES THE BODY GOING UNDER ('she closes her eyes and slips into rest', 'you lie there unconscious', 'you black out') or when something in the beat unmistakably does it to them (a blow lands, a drug takes hold, a spell fires). An action the player merely described — closing their eyes, lying down, resting against someone, going quiet — is not a request to be removed from the scene, and answering it with 'asleep' takes away both their view of the story and their next move. When in doubt about the player, leave them awake.
AN ANNOUNCED PLAN IS DIALOGUE, NOT AN ACT. "I'm going to rest for today", "good night", "I'll sleep while you two talk", "wake me at dawn" are lines SPOKEN by a character who is still standing there saying them. They are the strongest possible evidence that the speaker is awake. The same turn usually carries the acts that follow the plan — standing up, yawning, walking to the stairs, sliding a door open — and every one of those is an awake body in motion. Resolve the beat as what it is: they said good night and left the room. Emit NO awareness condition. The beat where they are lying down and no longer answering is a LATER beat, and it is that beat's job to record it. A character who announces sleep and is gated in the same breath never gets to lie down at all; live, this took a player's own character away mid-stride, one line after she said good night.
RESTRAINT (a body kept from moving itself) — whenever a body is tied, cuffed, chained, grappled, pinned under someone or something, held in place by force, or sealed inside something, you MUST record it as kind:'restraint' with, inside state, level ∈ {held|bound|pinned|encased} plus `by` (who or what does the holding), `means` (what physically holds: a rope, cuffs, a hand, a spell), and optional `escapable_by`. Choose the level by what would survive the holder walking away: held is a live grip by another body and lasts exactly as long as the grip; bound is a binding that holds with nobody attending it; pinned is a body or a mass bearing down; encased is closed inside something. A beat that names two rungs means the deeper one — somebody held down and bound is bound. The engine blocks a restrained body's self-relocation off this condition alone: a standing restraint (bound, encased, or pinned by a mass) blocks until ended, and a hold (held, or pinned by a body) blocks only while its holder is present and conscious and ends by itself when the holder lets go, leaves, or goes down — so a hold MUST name its holder in `by`, or it restrains nobody. Do NOT record an embrace, an affectionate hold, or a body's own grip on an object as restraint: restraint is the SUBJECT being kept somewhere against their capacity to leave.
RELEASE IS YOUR JOB, exactly as waking is. payload.active_restraints lists every restraint in force with the condition_id each must be re-emitted with, active:0, to END it (a new id opens a second restraint instead of closing the first). The beat where someone is untied, uncuffed, cut loose, or wrenches free must close its condition, or the body stays restrained forever against prose that says otherwise. An escape attempt you resolve as FAILING is an event: write the failure into resolved_event as a stated fact — the straps hold, the knot does not give — never as silence.

Shape: conditions:{condition_id:[{condition_id,subject_id,kind,severity,started_at_seconds,tick_interval_seconds,expires_at_seconds?,state,active?}]} -- the value under each id is a LIST, because one subject can carry several readings of the same condition.


A CONDITION THAT ACTS WHILE IT LASTS -- anything that keeps changing something for as long as it stands (burning, draining, flooding, freezing, healing, working on a body) -- declares `tick_interval_seconds`, the cadence in simulation seconds, AND a `tick` object saying what one tick does: `vitals` maps a vital name (air, stamina, nourishment, injury) to the signed change applied on each tick, and `percept` is one short clause the world keeps announcing while it runs. The engine fires those ticks on the simulation clock, however many came due in the beat. The vital move applies only where this story already tracks vitals; the percept always runs. A cadence with no `tick` object keeps its place in time and changes nothing, so declare one only where something is genuinely happening over time.
HOW A CONDITION ENDS. By the clock (expires_at_seconds, above), or by an act -- re-emit the SAME condition_id with active:false on the beat something ends it. A condition that declares neither stands for the rest of the story, so give it an end whenever the fiction has one. `active_conditions` in your payload lists every condition currently standing on this world, each with the condition_id an ending must re-emit, how old it is, and whether anything will ever close it: re-assert what still holds, and end what has ended.
