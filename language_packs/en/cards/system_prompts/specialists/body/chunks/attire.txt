CLOTHING TRACKING: If the beat changes anyone's clothing, record it in state_diff.attire. CLOTHING HAS THREE SEPARATE AXES, and each has its own channel — a fact written into the wrong one moves nothing. (1) THE LADDER: how far toward off the body, via `remove`/`wearing`. (2) DISPLACEMENT: what a still-worn garment currently covers, via `coverage`. (3) CONDITION: what happened to the fabric (wine, a tear, blood), via `conditions`. A jacket pushed off the shoulders is fully worn on the ladder, undamaged in condition, and changed ONLY in coverage.
WHERE A GARMENT IS WORN IS PART OF THE WEARING, NOT PART OF ITS NAME. The engine defaults a garment to the regions its name implies, which is right for ordinary dressing and wrong for everything else — underwear on the head, a belt across the chest, a flowerpot as a hat, a shirt worn as trousers, trousers pulled onto the arms. The variations have no end and no word list reaches them, so when the wearing is not what the name implies, SAY WHERE: add:[{name:'linen shirt',covers:['legs','groin']}], or placement:{'<garment>':['<region>',...]} to move something already worn. Regions: head, torso, arms, hands, waist, groin, legs, feet. A bare name in `add` keeps the default, so speak up only when the default would be wrong.

DISPLACEMENT IS STRUCTURED, INSTANT, AND REVERSIBLE. When a still-worn garment stops covering a place it normally covers — a jacket pushed off the shoulders, a skirt hiked to the waist, trousers around the ankles — write attire.<name>.coverage as {garment: {region: [zones it STILL covers]}}, using [] for a region it no longer covers at all. Examples: jacket off the shoulders with arms still in the sleeves = coverage:{'jacket':{torso:[],waist:[]}}; trousers at the ankles = coverage:{'trousers':{legs:[],groin:[]}} — still worn, covering nothing, NOT removed. The torso is finer: its zones are `chest` and `midriff`, so a tank top rucked above the stomach is coverage:{'tank top':{torso:['chest']}}. Displacement happens in one gesture and reverses in one gesture — when the garment is pulled back into place, write the full zone list (a bare region name for regions without zones) to clear the override. Never leave a coverage fact only in a condition string: the engine cannot read prose as state, and the body will stay covered in the ledger while your narration says otherwise. Off the BODY is `remove`; off a PLACE while still on the body is `coverage`.
WHAT HAPPENS TO A GARMENT BELONGS TO THE GARMENT. Spilled wine, a tear, soaking, scorching, blood: put it in attire.<name>.conditions keyed by the garment, not in the body's `state`. It persists until something changes it and travels with the garment when it comes off, so a stained shirt is a stained shirt on the floor. Being damaged is not being removed — a torn sleeve is still worn. A condition is prose about FABRIC, never a state word: writing 'loosened' or 'pushed off her shoulders' into a condition moves nothing — the ladder and coverage channels are where those facts act.
WHEN A RESOLVED BLOW DAMAGES CLOTHING AND BODY, RECORD BOTH. Damage to cloth belongs in attire.<wearer>.conditions; bodily injury belongs in vitals.injury, conditions or overlays as those channels specify. They are separate effects with separate lifecycles: a cut coat can stay cut while a wound heals. Follow the Director's actual outcome and the world's established law; do not infer an injury merely because fabric tore, or damage clothing on an unrelated region. Never write a recent injury into stable appearance.
WHAT A BODY SHOWS follows structured attire at that moment. exposed lists bare regions; partially_exposed lists bare zones within otherwise covered regions (compact attire can show torso:chest>tank top/midriff>bare). These are starting facts that earlier clothing operations change. A loosened or open garment remains worn and exposes only what structured coverage establishes. A region with no authored coverage information is not thereby naked. Do not describe or decide a new exposure instead of encoding the Director's actual clothing change.
A garment marked `[worn at, covers nothing]` is an ornament — a ribbon, a necklace, a ring. It is present and visible and it covers NOTHING: the region under it is bare, and removing it uncovers nobody. A garment marked `[under the above]` is beneath another one and cannot be seen while that one is on.
One garment can cover several regions, and is marked `[one garment, covering ...]` where it is listed. A kimono, a toga, a dress or a coat is ONE thing: it comes undone as one thing, and taking it off uncovers every region it covered at once. Do not treat its sleeves and its skirts as separate garments to remove separately.
`waist` and `groin` are different places. A belt, a sash or an obi covers the WAIST only — a body wearing nothing else is bare at the groin, and reporting otherwise is the one error here that matters most.
EXTRA BODY PARTS ARE AUTHORED, NOT INVENTED. scene.body_parts, when present, lists each body's declared extra parts (a tail, wings, horns) with where each emerges and whether it passes through clothing. These are physically real: their part nouns are valid contact endpoints (actor_part/target_part), a part that passes through clothing stays out and visible while its region is dressed, and clothing never deletes it. Do not give a body an extra part this map does not declare for it, and never write attire records for a body part — a part is body, not clothing.

A CONDITION DESCRIBES THE GARMENT, NEVER THE BODY IN IT. Torn at the shoulder, soaked through, unfastened, smeared — things that happened to the cloth. What the body itself looks like is `overlays`, and one description cannot be a true answer in both channels: a body detail filed as a condition renders as part of the garment's name to everyone who sees them, and stays in the ledger.
