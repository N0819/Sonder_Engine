ROOM CREATION: If anyone moves to a room that does not yet exist, create it in state_diff.rooms, with an adjacency edge joining it to the room the mover came from. A DOORWAY IS ONE DOORWAY, declared from either end. The room graph is undirected: an edge you write on the new room is equally an edge of the room it names, and you never have to write it twice. A passage that can only be crossed ONE WAY -- a chute, a drop, a hatch that latches behind you -- says so with `passage_from:'<room_id>'` on the edge, naming the one room a body may cross FROM; write it from either end or both, and both must name the same room. LEAVING THE EDGE OFF ONE SIDE DOES NOT MEAN ONE-WAY and never has -- it means you only wrote it once, which is the ordinary way to write it.

BARRIERS ARE A CLOSED SET THE ENGINE READS: `barrier` on an edge is exactly one of open, open_door, closed_door, window (see through, never pass -- glass, a sealed port), bars (see AND hear through, never pass -- a grille, a cell front), one_way_window (sight passes ONE WAY; declare the edge from both rooms and put `sight_from:'<room_id>'` on it naming the room that can see through, or neither room sees), membrane (a body passes, sight NEVER does -- a curtained doorway, a tent flap, the yielding wall of something climbed inside), wall. That is the whole vocabulary; sight, sound and passage are all derived from it and from nothing else you write. What the passage IS goes in the edge's `name` -- a shoji, a hatch, a stair, a bead screen is a name with one of those eight as its barrier. A word from outside the set is folded where it can be and becomes a WALL where it cannot, which seals a doorway you meant to open.

ZONES: Only when a scene introduces a genuinely DISCONNECTED locale — a second starship, a distant city, anywhere that isn't reachable by walking through this location's own rooms — set state_diff.rooms.room_id.zone to a short slug shared by every room of that locale (e.g. 'starship_beta'). Leave zone unset on ordinary rooms of the SAME location, even ones with no adjacency path mapped yet — zone is reserved for actual separate places, not unmapped hallways. Two parties standing in differently-zoned rooms may be split onto independently-advancing tracks by the engine; do not set this to force a dramatic effect.

CROSSING TIME: a room that is passed THROUGH rather than stayed in may carry `transit_seconds` -- how long crossing it takes, in story seconds. The engine then carries an occupant one place further along on the clock, without anyone declaring the move. Set it on a place whose whole point is that it is traversed, and leave it unset on a place that is occupied; absent means the room holds whoever is in it until something moves them. The magnitude IS the declaration, so a slow traverse is a large number rather than a different kind of room.

HOW BIG IT IS: `size` is one of tiny, small, medium, large, huge, vast -- an ORDERED rank, not a description, and the thing that decides whether two bodies standing at different anchors of one room are near each other or across it from each other. Set it on a room the first time you write it. Absent, the engine grades the room from its own name and otherwise treats it as medium; a word outside those six is not a size and is discarded, so a room written `enormous` is indistinguishable from a room nobody ever sized.

LIGHT: every room carries `light` — dark | dim | lit | bright — and it is what there is to SEE BY, not decoration. Absent means ordinary light. Set it when the fiction changes it: a lamp goes out, a door shuts on the only window, a flare is lit, power fails, dusk falls. IN A DARK ROOM NOBODY SEES ANYTHING — not the room, not the person standing beside them, not who just spoke — so an act that needs sight fails there, and characters navigate by sound and touch instead. Dim means shapes and movement without detail: you can tell someone is there, not who they are, unless you already know their voice or step. Light spills: a dark room with an open way through to a lit one is dim rather than pitch black, and the engine works that out — you only set the room's own light. Never quietly let a character see in the dark; if they can, it is because of something they ARE (a sense in their sheet, a light they are carrying), and that belongs on the page.

AN OCCUPIED BODY IS A PLACE, AND `parent_entity` IS WHAT MAKES IT ONE. When the beat puts one body inside another body's own interior, that inside is rooms like any other rooms: create them in state_diff.rooms with `parent_entity` set to the enclosing entity's id, connect their stations to one another with barrier membrane, and write the occupant's position to whichever station the beat puts them at. That one field is the whole of what makes a room an inside, and nothing else does it: with it, whoever is in that room is hidden from the room the body stands in, reaches the world only through that body, and travels wherever that body goes; without it the place is somewhere in the world, however enclosed your own description of it sounds. `exposure` says whether weather reaches a place, `light` how much can be seen there, and `desc` what it is like to be in it — none of the three puts anybody inside anybody. The enclosing body keeps its own separate position out in the world. Never author the way in and out: it is derived from the entity's own state, and an edge you write to the room that body is standing in is a sightline the enclosure was supposed to close. This is not a special case — an inside a body can be moved through is geography, and until it is rooms nothing in the engine can say where in there anyone is — and it is the field whose absence is invisible, because the place reads correctly everywhere it is described while the body in it stands exposed to everyone around its host.

A ROOM YOU ARE HANDED AS PLANNED EXISTS AND IS YOURS TO FURNISH. payload.planned_rooms, when present, lists rooms the location's plan laid out that no beat has yet described -- the one a body stands in, the one it is moving into, the ones in view or behind a door beside it -- each with its purpose and its exits. Its purpose and its exits are given; its contents are not. Write the room into rooms by its id the first time a body enters it or has it in view, whether or not the prose described it: a desc that serves the stated purpose, its light, and its anchors with their bearings and heights. You may add exits; the plan's exits stay, and the engine restores one you drop. A room you were not handed is not a stub and needs nothing from this.

AN ANCHOR IS A THING WITH A HEIGHT, and its height is what decides who can see past it. Every anchor may carry `footprint` (point | small | large | run -- how much of the room it takes), `height` (floor | waist | head | full -- what a line of sight is cut at) and `opacity` (opaque | see_through). The engine places each anchor from its `dir` and casts sight across the room from where each body stands, so a counter written `waist` hides a body crouched behind it and shows one standing behind it from the waist up, a screen written `head` hides a standing body, and a thing written `floor` or `see_through` hides nothing. Absent, an anchor blocks no line -- so a feature that could ever stand between two people is one whose height you state.

Shape: rooms:{room_id:{name,desc,adjacent:[{to,barrier,distance,vertical?,dir?,name?,passage_from?}],notes,zone?,size?,light?,exposure?,anchors?:{anchor_id:{desc,dir,footprint?,height?,opacity?}},parent_entity?,transit_seconds?}} -- a redeclared room merges onto the existing record by id; existing edges are preserved and only matched/updated by their 'to' room id.
