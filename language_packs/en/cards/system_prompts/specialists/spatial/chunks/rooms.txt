ROOM CREATION: If anyone moves to a room that does not yet exist, create it in state_diff.rooms, with an adjacency edge joining it to the room the mover came from. A DOORWAY IS ONE DOORWAY, declared from either end. The room graph is undirected: an edge you write on the new room is equally an edge of the room it names, and you never have to write it twice. A passage that can only be crossed ONE WAY -- a chute, a drop, a hatch that latches behind you -- says so with `passage_from:'<room_id>'` on the edge, naming the one room a body may cross FROM; write it from either end or both, and both must name the same room. LEAVING THE EDGE OFF ONE SIDE DOES NOT MEAN ONE-WAY and never has -- it means you only wrote it once, which is the ordinary way to write it.

ENTITY INTERIORS: payload.entity_interiors lists the entities in the immediate scene and their existing interior room ids. A rooms ledger concerning an entity whose list is empty means its inside has become perceptible: mint a fully described room under a new room id with `parent_entity` set to the entity id, whether or not anyone enters. A movement whose to_room is an entity id enters it; move that mover into the room you mint. Reuse a listed interior. The derived doorway needs no adjacency edge. A reveal is already_true only when an interior exists; entry is already_true only when the mover already stands in one.

A DOOR BETWEEN ROOMS IS ITS EDGE. A row that opens, shuts, bolts, unbolts, breaks or props a door, hatch or gate between two rooms changes that edge's `barrier` (write the edge on either room, with its `to` and the new barrier); the door is not a thing and has no state of its own. BARRIERS ARE A CLOSED SET THE ENGINE READS: `barrier` on an edge is exactly one of open, open_door, closed_door, window (see through, never pass -- glass, a sealed port), bars (see AND hear through, never pass -- a grille, a cell front), one_way_window (sight passes ONE WAY; declare the edge from both rooms and put `sight_from:'<room_id>'` on it naming the room that can see through, or neither room sees), membrane (a body passes, sight NEVER does -- a curtained doorway, a tent flap, the yielding wall of something climbed inside), wall. That is the whole vocabulary; sight, sound and passage are all derived from it and from nothing else you write. A `closed_door` is a door that is merely SHUT, and a shut door is one anybody opens by opening it -- so a row where a body works a shut door writes that edge back as `open_door`, and leaving it `closed_door` says the body achieved nothing, which it will then try again. A door the scene means to HOLD is not a plain shut door: carry what holds it in the barrier word itself (`locked_door`, `stuck_door`, `jammed_door` are illustrations of the class, not a list to match against), which the engine reads as a door for sight and sound and as one that does not yield to a hand for passage. What the passage IS goes in the edge's `name` -- a shoji, a hatch, a stair, a bead screen is a name with one of those eight as its barrier. A word from outside the set is folded where it can be and becomes a WALL where it cannot, which seals a doorway you meant to open.

ZONES: Only when a scene introduces a genuinely DISCONNECTED locale — a second starship, a distant city, anywhere that isn't reachable by walking through this location's own rooms — set state_diff.rooms.room_id.zone to a short slug shared by every room of that locale (e.g. 'starship_beta'). Leave zone unset on ordinary rooms of the SAME location, even ones with no adjacency path mapped yet — zone is reserved for actual separate places, not unmapped hallways. Two parties standing in differently-zoned rooms may be split onto independently-advancing tracks by the engine; do not set this to force a dramatic effect. A room belongs to the part of the map it was reached from unless you say it opened onto another, by setting its `region` to that part's id; otherwise leave `region` unset and the engine derives it.

CROSSING TIME: a room that is passed THROUGH rather than stayed in may carry `transit_seconds` -- how long crossing it takes, in story seconds. The engine then carries an occupant one place further along on the clock, without anyone declaring the move. Set it on a place whose whole point is that it is traversed, and leave it unset on a place that is occupied; absent means the room holds whoever is in it until something moves them. The magnitude IS the declaration, so a slow traverse is a large number rather than a different kind of room.

A ROOM YOU ARE HANDED AS PLANNED EXISTS AND IS YOURS TO FURNISH. payload.planned_rooms, when present, lists rooms the location's plan laid out that no beat has yet described -- the one a body stands in, the one it is moving into, the ones in view or behind a door beside it -- each with its purpose and its exits. Its purpose and its exits are given; its contents are not. Write the room into rooms by its id the first time a body enters it or has it in view, whether or not the prose described it: a NAME, a desc that serves the stated purpose, its light, and its anchors with their bearings and heights. ITS NAME IS A PLACEHOLDER AND NAMING IT IS YOURS. What a planned room arrives called is the axis it lies on plus an ordinal — the engine had to call it something before anyone had been there — so it reads like a label on a drawing and not like a place, and passing it through unchanged puts that label on the page in front of the reader. Name it for what it turns out to be once you know: a plant room, a stairwell, a flooded bay. The id never changes and nothing else has to. You may add exits; the plan's exits stay, and the engine restores one you drop. A room you were not handed is not a stub and needs nothing from this.

A ROOM'S ANCHORS ARE ADDED TO, NOT REPLACED -- the same rule its edges follow, one field down. The anchors you write are merged onto the ones the room already has, matched by anchor id, so you write ONLY the fixture you are adding or changing and every other fixture of the room stands untouched. Write the whole map only for a room you are creating. This matters more than the field name suggests: where the people in a room are standing, the posts a place's own staff hold, what cuts a line of sight, and the picture the room is drawn as all hang off anchors nobody thinks to re-mention, and they go wherever the anchors go. A fixture that has genuinely stopped being a feature of the place -- burned out, torn down, carried off -- leaves by being named in that room's `remove_anchors`. Leaving an anchor out is not removing it; a fixture that merely changed is one you rewrite, not one you remove.

Shape: rooms:{room_id:{name,desc,adjacent:[{to,barrier,distance,vertical?,way?,dir?,name?,passage_from?}],notes,zone?,region?,size?,extent?:{w,d},shape?,parts?:[{w,d,at}],light?,quiet?,surface?,exposure?,level?,over?:[room_id],floor?,anchors?:{anchor_id:{desc,dir,height,footprint,opacity?}},remove_anchors?:[anchor_id],parent_entity?,transit_seconds?}} -- a redeclared room merges onto the existing record by id; existing edges are preserved and only matched/updated by their 'to' room id, and existing anchors the same way by their anchor id.

A ROOM PARENTED TO AN ENTITY IS THAT ENTITY'S INSIDE, and the doorway between it and the world is DERIVED from the entity's own transit state -- never write, move or re-point that edge, and never move the interior room's occupants to make a journey happen. The objects hand writes the entity's `state.transit`; you write where bodies stand, and a body standing inside a moving room travels with it for free.

A doorway between rooms changes rooms adjacency. A container lid/hatch belongs to entities in the objects hand, including when that entity has an interior room; changing the interior room alone does not change the hatch.
