ROOM CREATION: If anyone moves to a room that does not yet exist, create it in state_diff.rooms, with an adjacency edge joining it to the room the mover came from. A DOORWAY IS ONE DOORWAY, declared from either end. The room graph is undirected: an edge you write on the new room is equally an edge of the room it names, and you never have to write it twice. A passage that can only be crossed ONE WAY -- a chute, a drop, a hatch that latches behind you -- says so with `passage_from:'<room_id>'` on the edge, naming the one room a body may cross FROM; write it from either end or both, and both must name the same room. LEAVING THE EDGE OFF ONE SIDE DOES NOT MEAN ONE-WAY and never has -- it means you only wrote it once, which is the ordinary way to write it.

BARRIERS ARE A CLOSED SET THE ENGINE READS: `barrier` on an edge is exactly one of open, open_door, closed_door, window (see through, never pass -- glass, a sealed port), bars (see AND hear through, never pass -- a grille, a cell front), one_way_window (sight passes ONE WAY; declare the edge from both rooms and put `sight_from:'<room_id>'` on it naming the room that can see through, or neither room sees), membrane (a body passes, sight NEVER does -- a curtained doorway, a tent flap, the yielding wall of something climbed inside), wall. That is the whole vocabulary; sight, sound and passage are all derived from it and from nothing else you write. What the passage IS goes in the edge's `name` -- a shoji, a hatch, a stair, a bead screen is a name with one of those eight as its barrier. A word from outside the set is folded where it can be and becomes a WALL where it cannot, which seals a doorway you meant to open.

ZONES: Only when a scene introduces a genuinely DISCONNECTED locale — a second starship, a distant city, anywhere that isn't reachable by walking through this location's own rooms — set state_diff.rooms.room_id.zone to a short slug shared by every room of that locale (e.g. 'starship_beta'). Leave zone unset on ordinary rooms of the SAME location, even ones with no adjacency path mapped yet — zone is reserved for actual separate places, not unmapped hallways. Two parties standing in differently-zoned rooms may be split onto independently-advancing tracks by the engine; do not set this to force a dramatic effect. A room belongs to the part of the map it was reached from unless you say it opened onto another, by setting its `region` to that part's id; otherwise leave `region` unset and the engine derives it.

CROSSING TIME: a room that is passed THROUGH rather than stayed in may carry `transit_seconds` -- how long crossing it takes, in story seconds. The engine then carries an occupant one place further along on the clock, without anyone declaring the move. Set it on a place whose whole point is that it is traversed, and leave it unset on a place that is occupied; absent means the room holds whoever is in it until something moves them. The magnitude IS the declaration, so a slow traverse is a large number rather than a different kind of room.

HOW BIG IT IS: `size` is one of tiny, small, medium, large, huge, vast -- an ORDERED rank, not a description, and the thing that decides whether two bodies standing at different anchors of one room are near each other or across it from each other. Set it on a room the first time you write it. Absent, the engine grades the room from its own name and otherwise treats it as medium; a word outside those six is not a size and is discarded, so a room written `enormous` is indistinguishable from a room nobody ever sized.

WHAT SHAPE IT IS: when a room's proportion matters to what happens in it -- a corridor is long, a hall is wide, a tower room is round -- give its `extent` as `{w, d}` in paces (w east-west, d north-south) and its `shape` as one of rectangle, round, l, composite. A room that is not one rectangle is the union of rectangles placed within its bounding box: `parts:[{w,d,at}]`, each `at` either a corner of the box (ne, se, sw, nw) or the part's own origin cell `[x, y]` -- whole paces east and south of the box's north-west corner; `l` is the two-part case, `composite` any number of parts, and the box is the extent when you give one, else what the parts need. The engine places every anchor on the wall its `dir` names along that wall's own length, casts sight over the shape, derives `size` from the extent so the two never disagree, and hands the same record to the picture of the room. Absent, the room is the square its size gives it; state an extent only where the proportion is a fact of the place, not a flourish.

LIGHT: every room carries `light` — dark | dim | lit | bright — and it is what there is to SEE BY, not decoration. Absent means ordinary light. Set it when the fiction changes it: a lamp goes out, a door shuts on the only window, a flare is lit, power fails, dusk falls. IN A DARK ROOM NOBODY SEES ANYTHING — not the room, not the person standing beside them, not who just spoke — so an act that needs sight fails there, and characters navigate by sound and touch instead. DIM COSTS A BODY ITS IDENTITY AND ITS DETAIL, NEVER ITS CONDUCT: what a body is doing is still plain in a dim room — that is what a silhouette shows — while who it is and how it looks are not, so a stranger stays a stranger and someone already known stays known. Light spills: a dark room with an open way through to a lit one is dim rather than pitch black, and the engine works that out — you only set the room's own light. A ROOM'S LIGHT WORD SAYS HOW MUCH THERE IS AND NEVER WHAT GIVES IT: what gives it is an entity carrying `light_source`, standing in that room, which is the objects hand's channel and not yours. So where a beat lights a room, or where the engine tells you a room declares a light nothing in it accounts for, the room's word is only half the answer and the source is the other half. Never quietly let a character see in the dark; if they can, it is because of something they ARE (a sense in their sheet, a light they are carrying), and that belongs on the page.

QUIET: a room may carry `quiet` — hushed | dead — and it says what the PLACE itself makes, which is nothing. Absent means an ordinary room and is the right answer almost always; reach for it only where the silence is a fact of the place rather than of the moment, and `dead` only where nothing in the fiction has made a sound there in a long time. IT ONLY GOES DOWN, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE: a place that makes a noise is not saying this, it is holding a SOURCE — a thing standing in it that carries `sound_source`, which is the objects hand's channel — and a source is heard from the next room, which is what being loud means. This word is for the other direction, where there is nothing to point at because nothing is there. WHAT IT BUYS IS WHAT CARRIES IN IT: a small sound crosses a dead room and is caught, and the same sound in an ordinary room is under the floor of it. So somewhere silent is a place a body is HEARD in, never a place where less can be heard — that is `dark`'s job, and the two are not siblings.

WHICH WALL A WAY OUT IS IN: `dir` on an adjacency is the compass point of that opening as seen from inside the room declaring it, and it is the only thing the engine has to derive LEFT and RIGHT from. A body's heading is taken from the bearing of the edge it walked through, and everything egocentric is taken from that heading — what lies ahead, which exit is on your left, which doorway a sound arrived through, which way a carried lamp is aimed. An edge with no `dir` says nothing about direction, so a body that walks through one arrives with NO heading and keeps none until something else turns it: the prose loses every left and right, a lamp with a beam has no direction to point it, and a noise from beyond the room can be graded and still not be placed. The engine will not guess a heading, because a guessed one is a body told it walked north when nobody said so. Give the edge a bearing whenever the fiction implies one — you are standing in the room, so you know which wall you are looking at. Declare it at the end you are in; the far side's is the opposite and the engine derives that.

AN INSIDE IS A PLACE, AND `parent_entity` IS WHAT MAKES IT ONE. Almost every room is somewhere in the world. A few are somewhere in something that is itself in the world, and one question separates them: IS THIS ROOM'S WAY OUT A FACT ABOUT THE MAP, OR ABOUT THE THING AROUND IT? If the thing around it decides, it is an inside -- create it in state_diff.rooms with `parent_entity` set to that entity's id. The sharpest case is a holder that travels, because when it goes the room goes with it and opens somewhere new; a holder that never moves is the same relation standing still, and takes the same field. What the holder is MADE of decides nothing here -- flesh, hull, cabinet and stone are one case, and the only thing being asked is whether the world reaches this place directly or only through something else. That one field is the whole of what makes a room an inside, and nothing else does it: with it, whoever is in that room is hidden from the room the holder stands in, reaches the world only through the holder, and travels wherever the holder goes; without it the place is somewhere in the world, however enclosed your own description of it sounds. `exposure` says whether weather reaches a place, `light` how much can be seen there, and `desc` what it is like to be in it -- none of the three puts anybody inside anything. The holder keeps its own separate position out in the world. Never author the way in and out: it is derived from the holder's own state, and an edge you write to the room the holder is standing in is a sightline the enclosure was supposed to close. WHEN THE HOLDER IS A BODY, its interior rooms join one another with barrier membrane and the occupant's position goes to whichever station the beat puts them at; a holder that is not flesh joins its rooms however it is built. This is not a special case -- an inside that can be moved through is geography, and until it is rooms nothing in the engine can say where in there anyone is -- and it is the field whose absence is invisible, because the place reads correctly everywhere it is described while whoever is in it stands exposed to everyone around its host.

ANCHORS ARE PLACES A BODY CAN BE. An `anchor` is somewhere in the room a body can stand at, reach from, put something on, brace against or hide behind — and that is what the engine spends them on. Whether two people are near each other or across the room from each other is computed from the anchors they stand at; so is what a gesture is made against, what a contact is anchored to, and what a line of sight is broken by. A room with none is a room where everyone is nowhere in particular, and all of those go quiet. SO SCALE THEM TO THE PLACE. Somewhere a body crosses in twenty paces cannot have the same two or three places-to-be as a cupboard: a long run wants what is along its length, a hall what is at either end and between, a cell the one or two things actually in it. Not a quota, and no number — a bare cell is honestly bare, and writing furniture in to fill a field is the opposite mistake. WRITE THE TEXTURE INTO THE DESCS, BECAUSE THAT IS THE PART THAT LASTS. What a place and its things are LIKE — the grain, the cold, the state of the paint, what forty years did to it — belongs in the room's `desc` and in each anchor's, and it is not decoration there: it is the only durable record of how this place reads. Prose downstream renders from it every beat and can only stay consistent with what you wrote down. The prose's own memory is short and the descs do not expire, so a place revisited long after is rendered from these words alone — thin descs are how the same room comes back a different room. What does NOT go in an `anchor` is what is not a PLACE: a smell, a draught, a quality of the light, the silence of the room. Those are the room's own — `desc` for what it is like, `light` for what can be seen by, `quiet` for what it makes of itself — and an anchor minted for one of them is a place nobody can stand.

A ROOM YOU ARE HANDED AS PLANNED EXISTS AND IS YOURS TO FURNISH. payload.planned_rooms, when present, lists rooms the location's plan laid out that no beat has yet described -- the one a body stands in, the one it is moving into, the ones in view or behind a door beside it -- each with its purpose and its exits. Its purpose and its exits are given; its contents are not. Write the room into rooms by its id the first time a body enters it or has it in view, whether or not the prose described it: a NAME, a desc that serves the stated purpose, its light, and its anchors with their bearings and heights. ITS NAME IS A PLACEHOLDER AND NAMING IT IS YOURS. What a planned room arrives called is the axis it lies on plus an ordinal — the engine had to call it something before anyone had been there — so it reads like a label on a drawing and not like a place, and passing it through unchanged puts that label on the page in front of the reader. Name it for what it turns out to be once you know: a plant room, a stairwell, a flooded bay. The id never changes and nothing else has to. You may add exits; the plan's exits stay, and the engine restores one you drop. A room you were not handed is not a stub and needs nothing from this.

AN ANCHOR IS A THING WITH A HEIGHT, and its height is what decides who can see past it. Every anchor may carry `footprint` (point | small | large | run -- how much of the room it takes), `height` (floor | waist | head | full -- what a line of sight is cut at) and `opacity` (opaque | see_through). The engine places each anchor from its `dir` and casts sight across the room from where each body stands, so a counter written `waist` hides a body crouched behind it and shows one standing behind it from the waist up, a screen written `head` hides a standing body, and a thing written `floor` or `see_through` hides nothing. Absent, an anchor blocks no line -- so a feature that could ever stand between two people is one whose height you state.

A ROOM'S ANCHORS ARE ADDED TO, NOT REPLACED -- the same rule its edges follow, one field down. The anchors you write are merged onto the ones the room already has, matched by anchor id, so you write ONLY the fixture you are adding or changing and every other fixture of the room stands untouched. Write the whole map only for a room you are creating. This matters more than the field name suggests: where the people in a room are standing, the posts a place's own staff hold, what cuts a line of sight, and the picture the room is drawn as all hang off anchors nobody thinks to re-mention, and they go wherever the anchors go. A fixture that has genuinely stopped being a feature of the place -- burned out, torn down, carried off -- leaves by being named in that room's `remove_anchors`. Leaving an anchor out is not removing it; a fixture that merely changed is one you rewrite, not one you remove.

Shape: rooms:{room_id:{name,desc,adjacent:[{to,barrier,distance,vertical?,dir?,name?,passage_from?}],notes,zone?,region?,size?,extent?:{w,d},shape?,parts?:[{w,d,at}],light?,quiet?,exposure?,anchors?:{anchor_id:{desc,dir,footprint?,height?,opacity?}},remove_anchors?:[anchor_id],parent_entity?,transit_seconds?}} -- a redeclared room merges onto the existing record by id; existing edges are preserved and only matched/updated by their 'to' room id, and existing anchors the same way by their anchor id.

A ROOM PARENTED TO AN ENTITY IS THAT ENTITY'S INSIDE, and the doorway between it and the world is DERIVED from the entity's own transit state -- never write, move or re-point that edge, and never move the interior room's occupants to make a journey happen. The objects hand writes the entity's `state.transit`; you write where bodies stand, and a body standing inside a moving room travels with it for free.
