WITHIN-ROOM POSITION — state_diff.stations:{name:{at:anchor_id|null, near:[names]}} says where each body is INSIDE its room. `at` names one of that room's anchors (rooms carry anchors:{anchor_id:{desc,dir}}); `near` lists who is right beside them. Set a station whenever a beat moves someone within a room — onto the bed, to the hearth, away from the desk — and at:null when they step away from a fixture. A station persists until you change it. Stations are not contact: contact_ops says what a body is AGAINST, stations say WHERE IT IS, and being seated on, lying on, or pinned to something is a station AT it plus a contact WITH it plus that body's own pose snapshot. scene.stations in your payload is the current record — correct it whenever the fiction has moved someone and it still says otherwise.
WHICH SIDE OF THE FIXTURE: a body `at` an anchor stands on the room's side of it unless `cover` says otherwise -- `cover:true` (or the anchor's id) puts the body on the FAR side, between a counter and the wall, on the blind side of a screen, so the anchor stands between it and the room. Set it when the beat puts someone behind a thing rather than at it; the engine then decides from the anchor's height and each body's posture who can still see them, and payload.sightlines shows you what your stations come to.
A PLACE IS A CELL: payload.grid lays each room out as cells, one a pace, with every anchor's, doorway's and body's cell in it. A body that stops somewhere no anchor names -- the middle of the floor, three paces short of the door, beside the window -- is placed by `cell:[x,y]` in its room's grid; an anchor is a cell with a name, and `at` still says which. The engine walks declared movement over these cells and seats every arrival just inside the door it came through, so write a cell only where the fiction puts a body somewhere else. A body that fills a doorway -- stands in the opening, blocks it, holds the threshold -- has that door's own cell from grid.doors, and nobody passes it while it stands there.

An object's station locates it within its room. It does not change who carries it or which hand touches it: a held object can be at a fixture while the grip continues. A completed set-down also needs the objects hand to record the transfer, whose successful application ends the old bearing grip. Emit the station for the precise destination you own; do not treat it as the transfer or the release.

For a completed set-down, if objects is absent from this row's assigned_hands, include "inventory_ops" in result.required_channels alongside your desired station patch. A co_hands entry is not an assignment. Inventory handles carriage and the old bearing grip; request "contact_ops" for an independent contact change or a new grip. Request channels you need; do not emit their patches yourself.
