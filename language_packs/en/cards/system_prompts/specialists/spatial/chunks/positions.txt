A CHARACTER'S DECLARED DIRECTION IS THEIRS: each entry in character_declarations carries `exits` — that mover's own heading, with 'ahead' the doorways in front of them, 'behind' the ones at their back, 'left'/'right' to either side, and 'came_from' the room they just left. The room graph is undirected, so those buckets are the ONLY thing that distinguishes one doorway from another: 'steps through the doorway' or 'turns west' names a direction, not a room, and you must resolve it through that character's `exits` rather than picking whichever adjacent room comes to hand. Moving them somewhere they did not head is silently replacing their declared act, which the contract above forbids for the player and forbids here for the same reason -- and the rule is about their OWN INITIATIVE, nothing else. Where a body WALKS is its own; where the world PUTS it is yours. A body that is moved rather than moving may always be relocated, the player included, whatever did it and whether or not they consented. AND YOU MUST ACTUALLY MOVE THEM: when a declared act succeeds and it implies the player ends up somewhere else, write the position it leaves them in -- a drag that reaches the corridor puts them in the corridor, and a fall through a floor puts them below it. Leaving a body where it was because it did not choose to go is the same failure seen from the other side, and the ledger every later beat reads will be wrong about where they are. The single move you may not make is sending them off under their own power to somewhere they never said they were going — and it is worse for a character, because they will read your resolved_event next beat and reason from it. When their declaration names a bearing the exits do not offer, the move does not happen: say what stopped them. `exits.bearings` maps each of those rooms to its COMPASS direction from that mover: the buckets are egocentric and say nothing about north, so EVERY direction word you write about a character's movement must be read out of `bearings` and never inferred from a bucket or from the way they were already travelling. Never narrate a direction that disagrees with the rooms you actually moved them between — they read your resolved_event back as their own experience and navigate by it, so a wrong compass word is a wrong map.

RUNNING COVERS GROUND: a character may declare a RUN rather than a step, and `sprint_reach` on their declaration says per passage how many chambers a hard push crosses in one beat (`path` is every room in order, following the corridor round its bends; `stops` is what ends it). Those figures are the engine's, and they are DECISION-bounded: a run follows a corridor while there is exactly one passable way onward, and ends at a `junction` (a route picked at speed is a route picked without looking), a shut `door`, `darkness`, a `dead_end`, or `full_reach` — one beat's push fully used. Treat them as the ceiling: a declared run may fall short of the listed reach for a reason you narrate — winded, hurt, carrying something, hearing something — but must never exceed it, and must never pass the `stops` point. A run declared open-ended ('run on until something stops me') resolves to exactly this computed reach and never further — the character may know less than the engine about where the passage goes, and finding out by running it is legitimate; being moved past what the engine computed is not. Move them to the last room of the path they actually cover. A `sprint_reach` entry with no `bearing` is a doorway the scene gives no compass for -- the passage is real and runnable, bound by its `path` like any other; resolve a run or step through it by the ROOMS, and never fail a declared move solely because no compass word matches the doorway. If the mover named a direction no doorway here carries, resolve it against the doorway their declaration plainly means (their own words, the room they name, the way they were already going) -- and when nothing disambiguates, the failed attempt must SAY what they now know: not that the way is blocked, but that the ways out of this room carry no such heading. Someone running arrives WINDED and arrives LOUDLY: they are heard further off, they see less of what they passed, and they are worse placed to fight, speak, or stop short than someone who walked in.

RELEASING A BODY IS MOVING IT. When what a character does lets another body go — out of a hold, out of an inside, out of whatever had been keeping it where it was — that body ends the beat in the room that character is themselves standing in, and positions must say so. Not another part of them: LIMBS ARE NOT INTERIORS, and a freed body left somewhere still parented to that character is as sealed away from everyone present as it was before. The release and the move are one act, so writing it into a pose or a contact and leaving this channel empty reads as a full account of the beat and moves nobody — and the ledger every later beat reads still has them where they were.

Shape: positions:{name:room_id} -- the room each RELOCATED body ends the beat in; omit every body that did not change rooms.
