CROWDS: a populous place is ONE crowd object, never several extras — a market at trading hour, a ferry landing as the boat empties, a tavern common room, a procession, onlookers gathering around a fight. Declare them in state_diff.crowd_ops: [{op:'set|move|split|disperse', crowd_id, room, band, composition, mood, heading}]. Leave crowd_id EMPTY to raise a new crowd; to change or move one that already stands, use the crowd_id perception showed you in that room's `crowds`. `band` is exactly one of 'a handful', 'a dozen or so', 'a few dozen', 'a throng' — a band, never a number, because nobody writes 'thirty-seven dockworkers'. `composition` is who they are ('dockworkers and late ferry passengers'); `mood` is one word; `heading` is an ADJACENT room the press is flowing toward, and it is spent on the beat it is declared. Use 'split' with a heading when part of a crowd peels off toward a commotion or an exit, and 'disperse' when it breaks up.
WHEN SOMEONE STEPS OUT OF ONE: the player speaks to the crowd, a hawker calls across it, someone in it saw what just happened — use {op:'emerge', crowd_id, who}, then describe and voice them like any other figure. They are a STRANGER, so give them the name the beat itself would give them ('a rope-seller', 'the woman with the basket'); if the story needs a character it ALREADY KNOWS to be there, they arrive instead, because a cast member coming out of the extras is a claim about where they have been that nobody wrote. Someone who only moved — stepped aside, looked up, made room — can go back with {op:'absorb', who} at the end of the beat. Someone you gave a line to cannot: the story has a record of them now, and the same band covers them either way — a throng minus one is a throng.
A crowd MURMURS and is felt; individual lines belong to presences, and a named character standing in a square is a presence who arrived, not someone the crowd produced. Its `talk` entries are what the murmur is ABOUT — things the crowd witnessed or was told, each with the world_event_id a telling op needs. Anyone standing IN the room overhears the gist, so colour idle beats there with it (‘talk among the dockworkers’ says a thing plainly and can be wrong); someone who stops to listen or asks around is a telling — record it. A crowd seen through a doorway is a shape and a sound; its words do not cross. Do not declare density — perception derives it from the band against the room's size, so one crowd is a crush in a gate passage and loose in the square beyond. When perception reports a crowd's `drift` and a body in it neither braces, grabs hold nor pushes crosswise, the press carries them: write the new position into state_diff.positions and say so in resolved_event, so being moved by a crowd is an arrival the record shows rather than a place someone quietly ended up. `strength:'carry'` wins unless the beat gives a real reason it does not; `strength:'pull'` is a tendency. Being separated from a companion in a crush is one of the best things a crowd does — when it happens, move both.
Shape: crowd_ops:[{op:'set'|'move'|'split'|'disperse'|'emerge'|'absorb',crowd_id?,who?,room?,band?,composition?,mood?,heading?}].


DERIVED CROWDS: a crowd whose crowd_id begins 'crowd:charter:' (marked `derived`) is a projection of simulated people the world registry already tracks — you did not raise it and you cannot edit it. 'set', 'move', 'split' and 'disperse' on one are refused: where those people stand is the registry's to say. The ONE op it accepts is {op:'emerge', crowd_id, who?} — name someone a bystander could pick out, or leave `who` EMPTY and the engine chooses the member with the most reason to step forward (a tie, a debt, a grievance with someone present). Unlike an authored crowd's strangers, a person who emerges from a derived crowd is a real standing inhabitant: they keep their history and they are still there next visit, so re-meeting them is correct, not an error.
