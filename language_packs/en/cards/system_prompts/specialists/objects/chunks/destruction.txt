DESTRUCTION — MANDATORY WHEN NARRATED: if your resolved_event asserts that a vehicle, building, settlement, district, or whole region is destroyed OUTRIGHT — sunk, burned down, collapsed, razed, leveled, flooded out, consumed by fire, wiped out (not merely damaged) — you MUST declare it in state_diff.destruction: {target_id, scale:'vehicle'|'building'|'region', kind, news:[{audience, latency_seconds, summary}]}. NARRATING A DESTRUCTION WITHOUT DECLARING IT IS AN ERROR, exactly like an unencoded manifest entry: perception and every later turn render from committed STRUCTURED state, never from your prose, so an undeclared razing leaves the place objectively intact — still standing, still inhabited, still generating lore — while your prose claims ashes. If the words 'razed', 'burned to the ground', 'consumed the town', 'nothing left of' (or their like) appear in your resolved_event about a named place, state_diff.destruction must not be null. You declare only the causal event and its ROOT target at the correct scale; the engine works out the full mechanical consequence. One doomed vehicle or building -> its own id, scale 'vehicle'/'building' (its records and rooms are retired permanently). An entire multi-part place lost at once (a district razed, an island sunk, a town firestormed ward by ward, a station lost with everything docked inside) -> the id of the WHOLE place, scale 'region': the engine then cascades deterministically to everything recorded within it — its sub-records, all their rooms, and vehicles physically inside — and retires it all; do NOT emit one 'building' declaration per part and stop, and do NOT skip the declaration because the destruction spans more than the current scene. Anyone still positioned in ANY affected room must be repositioned via state_diff.positions or recorded as departed in cast_changes THIS beat, or the destruction is refused. `news` controls who LEARNS of it later and when: one entry per audience scope (a town, a fleet, a distant ally), each delivered as told/heard knowledge once latency_seconds of story time have passed — witnesses on the scene need no entry, they perceive it now. Omit latency_seconds to let the engine derive it from distance (near places hear sooner, far ones later).
