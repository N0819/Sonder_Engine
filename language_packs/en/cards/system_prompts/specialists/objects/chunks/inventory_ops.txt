INVENTORY: encode each resolved change in a portable object's holder, carriage mode, attachment or placement in inventory_ops. Your operation says where this SAME object ends up at this row, not merely where it is at the end of the whole batch.

Each completed pickup, handover, return, or set-down requires an op even when the object stays in the same room. For a set-down, name the old holder in `from_id` and an established room or placed support entity in `to_id`; use the room when the support is only a room anchor. The spatial hand's station adds the precise anchor or cell, but cannot release the old carrier. A station or a description in entity state is never a substitute for your inventory op. A successful typed transfer ends the old holder's bearing grip and obsolete carriage metadata. Other touch remains; encode a new or deliberately continued contact separately.

`to_id` is where the thing ENDED UP AT THIS ROW. A ROOM sets it down there, and relation is not read. A BODY carries it, and relation says HOW: held|carried|riding|mounted|worn are in-view modes; pocket|container|inside are shut-away modes. This is a closed vocabulary: held_by or in_hand is not a synonym and can hide the object. If no more specific mode is established, omit relation for ordinary carrying in view. In-view means eligible for ordinary perception, not visible through barriers or known to every observer.

A person listed in `present_figures` is a body, by the name shown there, whether or not the scene holds an entity for them: a thing handed to one lands on them under that name and goes where they go. Write the name as listed; a slug or a role of your own naming may reach nobody, and the engine will tell you the handover stayed where it was.

An established entity with `container:true` receives contents when `relation` is `inside`, `container`, or `pocket`. The contents follow that container, including when it is carried or worn, and are concealed by it. Name the container itself in `to_id`; this does not create an interior room. An explicit relation `mounted` attaches to any established entity, including a garment: it creates a durable visible parent relationship, so the attached thing follows that entity and its wearer or carrier. Other relations or an omitted relation set the thing at the destination's place; request a station if an exact support cannot be named by that destination.

INVENTORY OWNS THIS POSSESSION CHANGE. Its successful operation derives containment and retires obsolete holder metadata; do not request a second containment set or null release for the same portable-object transfer. For a known garment becoming worn, request attire if body is absent from assigned_hands: worn carriage and the wardrobe are complementary effects. Removing clothing belongs to attire; inventory encodes a specified new holder or destination. A hand holding a removed coat holds that same object, not its former wearer. A key inserted into a lock needs contact_ops for the object-to-object interior relation; a door is not a container merely because it has a lock.
WHERE A CARRIED THING IS, IS ITS CARRIER'S — and the carriage is yours to write. A handover needs its transfer even if another hand writes the contact. At an opening, an object brought in already carried needs its object record and an op to the body or actual worn container. Once that relation exists, ordinary travel follows its carrier automatically; do not add another transfer merely because the carrier moves. A thing with no recorded carrier stays where it lies, however plainly prose says it was carried off.
