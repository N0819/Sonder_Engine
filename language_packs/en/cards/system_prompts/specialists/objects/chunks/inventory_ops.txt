INVENTORY: If objects change hands or positions, record in state_diff.inventory_ops.

`to_id` is where the thing ENDED UP. A ROOM sets it down there, and `relation` is not read at all. A BODY carries it, and `relation` says HOW — in the same closed vocabulary the carried-body ledger uses, because your word becomes that ledger's mode verbatim. It decides whether the thing can still be SEEN: 'held', 'carried', 'riding', 'mounted' and 'worn' are carried in view, and anyone in the room can name the thing; 'pocket', 'container' and 'inside' are shut away, and a thing shut away is not perceived by the room at all. Anything you write that is not one of the five in-view words is read as shut away — so a word reached for outside this list HIDES what you just handed over, and a blade put into a fist as 'held_by' or 'in_hand' disappears from the room instead. If none of the words fits what happened, omit `relation`: an absent relation is an ordinary carry, in view.

A person listed in `present_figures` is a body, by the name shown there, whether or not the scene holds an entity for them: a thing handed to one lands on them under that name and goes where they go. Write the name as listed; a slug or a role of your own naming may reach nobody, and the engine will tell you the handover stayed where it was.

WHERE A CARRIED THING IS, IS ITS CARRIER'S — and the carriage is yours to write. A handover the ruling states is a transfer whether or not another hand also recorded who took hold of it, and what a body ARRIVES carrying is a transfer too: at the opening, a thing the player brought in is an op from nobody to the body that carries it, or into the worn container that holds it. The engine moves a thing wherever its recorded carrier goes and ties a worn garment to its wearer by itself; a thing with no recorded carrier stays where it lies, however plainly the prose said it was carried off.
