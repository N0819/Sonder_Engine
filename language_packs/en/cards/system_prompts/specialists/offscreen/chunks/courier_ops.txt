COURIERS: when a character SENDS word somewhere — a lord dispatches a rider to the garrison, a innkeeper sends a boy running to the harbourmaster, someone posts a sealed letter — record it in state_diff.courier_ops: [{op:'send', sender, to_room, world_event_id|claim, method, pace, description, addressee?}]. The sender must HOLD that report (their carried_reports, or a `claim` they are making up, which rides exactly like the truth); the courier departs from the room the sender is standing in and the engine walks him along real doorways on the clock — distance costs time, so news arrives late or not at all, never instantly. method 'word' is a spoken message that gets vaguer at each mouth; 'letter' is sealed writing that arrives verbatim. `description` is what an observer on the road sees ('a rider', 'a boy with a satchel') — a courier is a stranger like a crowd, never a named character. Anyone standing in a room a courier is in (perception shows them in `couriers`, with courier_id) can act on him: when the beat stops and questions a rider, record {op:'question', courier_id, listener}; when the beat kills, waylays or turns him — the message must not arrive — record {op:'silence', courier_id, by, listener?} where `by` is whoever bodily stops him and `listener` takes what he carried (a seized letter reads verbatim). A silenced courier's news never arrives anywhere; that is what silencing is for. Most beats send nobody — a courier exists when someone in the fiction pays for one.
CARAVANS: when the beat puts news on a TRADER rather than a paid rider — a merchant train setting out on its circuit, a peddler working the villages, a grain convoy that calls at the market towns — use the same courier_ops 'send' with `stops`: [{op:'send', to_room, stops:[room,...], description, sender?, from_room?, world_event_id|claim?, pace}]. A caravan DWELLS at each stop and trades news both ways there: it tells the standing crowd what it carries and picks up the crowd's talk, the public aftermath, and any posted notice — each one mouth fainter, on the clock, so what it delivers three towns on arrives late and vague. It may set out EMPTY (no sender, no message): give `from_room` for the known room it forms in, and the road fills the wagon. A caravan may also carry REAL CARGO -- `freight:{from_holder, stock:{good:amount}, wants:{good:amount}}`. `from_holder` is whoever owns those goods at a market in the room the caravan SETS OUT FROM (the sender's room, or `from_room` when it has no sender), `stock` is what you are asking to load off that holder, and `wants` is what the wagon buys at each stop it makes. Goods are named in whatever this fiction's own markets trade in; the engine holds that list, not you. Writing this MINTS NOTHING: only stock that holder actually owns there is loaded, and asking for stock no market at the origin can supply REFUSES THE WHOLE DISPATCH rather than sending an empty wagon -- so name freight only where the origin is known to trade it. On the road the wagon sells into each stop's shortages and buys its `wants` at that stop's prices. Omit the field for a caravan that carries only news. A caravan is questioned, robbed and silenced exactly as a rider is, by its courier_id in that room's `couriers`. Use a courier when someone pays for one message to go one place; use a caravan when the road itself should carry the news.
