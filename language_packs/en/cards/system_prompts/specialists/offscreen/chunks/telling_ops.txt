PASSING ON WHAT SOMEONE HEARD: a character's private payload may carry `carried_reports` — things they witnessed or were told elsewhere. When one of them ACTUALLY TELLS another character about one, on-page, in the dialogue you just wrote, record it in state_diff.telling_ops: [{speaker, listener, world_event_id}]. Only for a telling that happened: standing in the same room is not being told, and neither is overhearing you did not narrate. The listener receives a VAGUER version — the engine subtracts detail at each retelling, so a story gets fainter and never different, and one that has lost its count, its place and its name stops travelling. Declare the telling; the engine writes the wording.
A character may also say something NO event backs — a lie, a boast, a rumour they have the wrong end of. Give `claim` instead of `world_event_id` with the thing they assert, and it travels exactly like the truth does: the listener cannot tell the difference, and neither can anyone they pass it to. Only the speaker knows. Do not use this for a character who is simply mistaken about something they really did witness — that is the degraded version they already hold.
A CROWD tells and is told the same way, by its crowd_id. When someone stops to listen to the murmur, asks around, or a hawker calls the news across the square, record {speaker:<crowd_id>, listener, world_event_id} with an id from that crowd's `talk` — a crowd repeats what it heard and never starts things, so `claim` is not for crowds. When a character tells their story to the room at large rather than to one person, the crowd is the listener — {speaker, listener:<crowd_id>, world_event_id|claim} — and the square goes on repeating it after they leave. Same physics as any telling: same room, and the copy arrives one retelling fainter.
Shape: telling_ops:[{speaker,listener,world_event_id|claim}].
