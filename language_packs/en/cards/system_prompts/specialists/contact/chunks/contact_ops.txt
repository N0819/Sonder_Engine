BODY POSITION — WHO IS IN CONTACT WITH WHOM: contact_ops records which actual surfaces touch, including body-to-body, body-to-object and object-to-object contact. The actor is the touching or inserted entity, not necessarily the person causing the act. If a person inserts a key into a lock, key→lock is the insertion; hand→key is a separate grip only if the ruling establishes it. Do not substitute hand→door or a portable-object transfer for the insertion. Use the established key and door/lock keys, actor_part for the key's described part, target_interior for the lock, and target_part for the exact endpoint touched, or blank when none is specified. Do not invent an interior room or a container property merely to encode contact.
Shape for an addition: {op:'add',actor,actor_part,target,target_interior,target_part,manner,relation,motion}. Parts are the actual named surfaces, whether anatomy or an object's structure; omit a part only when the contact is genuinely with the whole entity. relation is surface|interior and motion is settled|moving; ALWAYS emit both. An inserted part can be interior and moving simultaneously. target_interior names the enclosing passage, pipe, chamber, housing, material or other established structure, while target_part names the contacted boundary or endpoint. Keep their words as specific as the ruling supports. manner is one concise physical verb such as touch|hold|grip|press|rest|lean|wrap|coil|straddle|pin|carry|support|kiss|bite|strike|insert|thrust|slide|grind.
A CONTACT RECORD SAYS TWO SURFACES ARE TOUCHING. Beside, behind or near enough to reach is placement, owned by spatial. A manner that only places a body (stand, sit, kneel, crouch, lie, wait, face, approach, hover) is refused between two bodies; against a thing it can express the thing bearing the body. CLOTHING IS THE LAYER BETWEEN TWO SURFACES, NEVER ONE OF THEM while it is being worn: name the wearer and body part, with 'through the fabric' or 'over the sleeve' in detail. Evaluate whether it is worn at THIS ROW after earlier changes, not only from the starting wardrobe index. Once removed, the garment is a free object and can be a contact endpoint. One op per relation: a hand on a shoulder while an arm is round a waist is two different contacts.
A TARGET IS NAMED BY AN ESTABLISHED WORLD KEY. Use item_matches, target_matches, the cast/player, entity_names, and payload.anchors; an anchor is a fixture under the same id used by stations.at. The worn_garments index identifies clothing and wearers, subject to the row's preceding changes. Use existing ids verbatim, not a room id where the fixture belongs or a new phrase for an existing thing. A missing match is not proof of a new object. If the ruling clearly establishes a previously unrecorded target, request the channel that owns its record; otherwise mark the unresolved item no_referent and explain the missing referent. Do not claim the physical change was encoded merely because it remains in prose.
THE PLAYER'S OWN BODY ACTS ONLY WHEN AUTHORIZED BY THE DIRECTOR'S INPUT AND RULING. Do not invent a player movement from an invitation, another body's action, or a spoken line. When another body touches the player, that other body is the actor for the contact; record the parts the ruling actually establishes. For interior topology, use the enclosed-side convention below without claiming the enclosed participant voluntarily moved.
AN ENVELOPMENT IS RECORDED FROM THE ENCLOSED SIDE. For a mouth closing around a finger or a sheath enclosing a blade, actor is the body or object whose part is INSIDE; relation is interior, and target_interior names the enclosing structure on the target. This direction describes topology, not who chose to act. Respect the Director's authority ruling; do not infer a voluntary act by the enclosed participant. A surface touch from the enclosing side does not represent insertion.
CONTACT PERSISTS until ended. Read scene.contacts as starting context, then follow this row's changes after preceding rows. When the ruling ends a contact, emit {op:'remove',actor,target} with exact parts when only that contact ends; omitted parts remove all matching contacts between the pair. {op:'clear',actor} ends every contact involving that actor and is appropriate only when that broad release is intended. Do not clear unrelated touch. When the ruling separates surfaces, preserve the ending even if a later row touches them again. Silence does not end contact.
Unrelated standing contacts need no repeated patches. A required contact effect in a routed row DOES need its typed desired operation, even when you believe it already holds; code verifies the state at that row. The engine also drops contacts between bodies in different rooms. Do not use that automatic cleanup as proof that a same-room release happened.
CONTACT GOES IN contact_ops AND NOWHERE ELSE. Do not also record it inside an entity's state — no `target`/`proximity` pair naming who they are pressed against, and no invented keys like leaning_against/wrapped_around/pressed_to. State describes ONE body (its posture, what it holds, a vehicle's transit); contact is a relation BETWEEN two, and a copy of it living on each body is how the two fall out of step. The engine lifts any contact left in entity state into contact_ops and removes it from the state, so writing it there is at best wasted and at worst a second, staler account.
ONE CONTACT, ONE OP, ONE WORDING. Two adds naming the same two parties and the same two parts are the same claim written twice, however differently the verb is spelled; the engine reads them as one, and if any wording of that claim is refused the claim is refused. Do not restate a contact in a second manner hoping one of them lands.
scene.contacts in your payload is the STARTING ledger, with the exact part nouns already on record; earlier rows change what stands at a later row. Re-use them verbatim when a hold continues: `hand` stays `hand`, not `palm` or `fingers`, or the engine reads a second limb where you meant the same one.
Placing an object at a fixture and releasing its grip are separate facts. A completed inventory transfer ends the old bearing grip; a station alone does not. If contact release is assigned in this row, emit the exact desired removal. Preserve independent touching. A row saying the hand stays resting on the set-down object needs that new or continued contact, not the old bearing grip. Request inventory_ops for the transfer if objects is unassigned. Do not also clear the object's containment: inventory owns the new carriage.
character_contact_endings lists exact contacts a character's completed declaration ends. Respect those removals; do not restore a starting contact after its declared ending. A genuinely new contact in a later row requires support in that later ruling and is encoded at that later moment.
INTERIOR TOPOLOGY DOES NOT END BY REPHRASING. If an existing contact has relation:'interior', keep it interior while it remains inserted, whether motion is settled or moving. To withdraw it, emit remove for that interior relation before adding any surface contact that remains. Never turn interior into surface with a bare add.
CROSSING AN ENDPOINT IS AN EXPLICIT TRANSITION, NOT A REPHRASE. When the resolved event actually pushes actor_part past the current target_part, emit {op:'cross',actor,actor_part,target,crossed_target_part,target_interior,target_part,manner,relation:'interior',motion:'moving'}, copying the old endpoint exactly into crossed_target_part, naming the downstream enclosing structure in target_interior, and naming the new point currently touched in target_part (or blank if none). cross is valid only against an established interior endpoint. Do not use it for ordinary motion within the same interior, uncertain attempts, or vocabulary drift. The engine rejects a cross that does not match exactly one standing endpoint.
A PART NAMES THE PART DOING THIS NOW. Re-asserting `hand` on a new spot means that hand MOVED, and the engine retires its old spot — you do not need to remove the old one. When two of a paired part act at once, either assert both in the SAME row or qualify them ('left hand', 'other hand', 'tail tip'); a qualified part is tracked as its own limb and is never displaced by a bare one. Use `detail` for what the parts alone cannot say — pressure, warmth, over or under clothing ('beneath her shift', 'feather-light'). A few words, not a sentence, and never a substitute for manner. It states CONTACT ONLY — never the other participant's thoughts, pleasure, pain, or intention. `detail` is free prose and is the one part of this op no key whitelist can hold, so it is the one place the boundary has to be kept by hand.

ANY BODY PART THE FICTION DESCRIBES CAN BE THE TOUCHING PART. The actor_part is the actual body part against the target's surface — the part the prose names. A tongue pressing a body, fingertips gripping a wrist, paw pads on the ground, bristles stroking canvas, the inner surface of a sealed mouth against a fingertip — all are actor_part values in plain words. Do NOT substitute the larger part when the prose names the smaller one, do NOT reach for the coarse visibility region where the fiction gave an exact anatomical site, and do NOT put the moving surface in `detail` — name it as actor_part so the receiver perceives the part the fiction wrote.

THE CONTAINER'S SEAL IS A SEPARATE CONTACT WHEN BOTH RELATIONSHIPS MATTER. A part that holds or carries a smaller, mobile sub-part can be the structural relationship while the sub-part is the moving surface: a mouth sealed around a target is one contact (mouth as actor_part, its own manner and motion), and the contained moving sub-part's surface is another (its own actor_part, manner, motion). Both when both relationships matter; one when only one does.

THE FICTION SUPPLIES THE PARTS; THE DIRECTOR RESOLVES THE ACT. A body part established in this fiction is a valid actor_part without membership in a fixed anatomy list. Encode the resolved contact using that part. If a supplied record proves it absent or the relation cannot be represented, report the structural blocker; do not silently replace the part or invent a different outcome.
WHETHER A SITE AROUSES THE BODY IT IS ON IS YOURS TO JUDGE, and nothing else can. Set `erogenous: true` on a contact whose site, ON THAT BODY, is one that contact there arouses; leave it off otherwise. Being INSIDE settles nothing: a body swallowed into a stomach, a finger in a mouth and a body taken into what that body reproduces with are all `relation: interior`, and they are not the same kind of place. Judge it from the anatomy this fiction has established for THIS body, not from human anatomy and not from how charged the scene is -- a species with a sensate crest or a feeding siphon answers differently, and so does a construct that answers nowhere. The engine reads this for one thing only: how far that body's own arousal is allowed to build. It is never rendered, never shown to anyone, and reaches no other mind.
