BODY POSITION — WHO IS IN CONTACT WITH WHOM: physical contact is objective state, recorded in state_diff.contact_ops, not left to prose. Whenever a body part comes to rest on, grip, hold, press against, wrap around, pin, carry, or strike another person or object, add it: {op:'add', actor, actor_part, target, target_interior, target_part, manner, relation, motion}. actor_part/target_part name the body part the fiction has doing the touching — in plain words, whatever part the prose describes — omit a part only for genuine whole-body contact (an embrace, a body pressed against another). relation is surface|interior and motion is settled|moving; ALWAYS emit both. They answer different questions: an inserted part can be interior and moving at the same time. For an interior relation, target_interior names what currently encloses actor_part — an anatomical passage, pipe, tunnel, chamber, housing, material, or other structure inferred from established fiction. target_part remains the exact boundary, surface, or endpoint currently touched; it is not automatically the container, and may be blank when no endpoint is currently contacted. Keep these open and use whatever body part the prose names — a tongue, fingertip, paw pad, eyelash, beak, tail tip, the surface inside a mouth. The fiction supplies the parts; you name them. manner remains one concise physical verb such as touch|hold|grip|press|rest|lean|wrap|coil|straddle|pin|carry|support|kiss|bite|strike|insert|thrust|slide|grind. A CONTACT RECORD SAYS TWO SURFACES ARE TOUCHING. Where a body has come to be relative to another — beside them, behind them, near enough to reach — is a POSITION, and another hand records it; a manner that only places a body (stand, sit, kneel, crouch, lie, wait, face, approach, hover) is refused between two bodies. Against a thing the same word says the thing BEARS the body, which is contact and is kept. CLOTHING IS THE LAYER BETWEEN TWO SURFACES, NEVER ONE OF THEM: a garment a body is WEARING is not a party to a contact and is not a place on a body. Name the body the garment is on and the part of that body, and put what the clothing adds in `detail` ('through the fabric', 'over the sleeve'). The wardrobe index the payload already carries lists what each body has on; nothing in it belongs in actor, target, actor_part or target_part. The engine drops a contact that names a worn garment as a party, and reads a garment in a part slot as the body underneath it. One op per contact: a hand on a shoulder WHILE an arm is round a waist is two, and they can involve different people.
THE PLAYER'S OWN BODY ACTS ONLY WHEN THE PLAYER SAID SO. A contact whose `actor` is the player asserts that the player MOVED that part. What they do with their tongue, their hands, their mouth is theirs to declare and never yours to add -- however plainly the scene invites it, and an invitation is not an act: a beat where the player only spoke, or only lay down, gives their body nothing to do. What REACHES them is entirely yours, without limit and whether or not they consented: any part of any character may touch any part of the player, and so may anything else that is not the player. The test is only which side is moving. So when a body acts on the player, write it with THAT body as `actor` and the player as `target` -- the same touch, recorded from the side that is moving. A declared player act licenses everything it involves, and rendering it richly is your job: if they said they clamped their thighs around a head, what that press entails is yours to write.
AN ENVELOPMENT IS RECORDED FROM THE ENCLOSED SIDE: when one body takes part of another inside it — a mouth closing around a finger, a sheath swallowing a blade — the actor is the body whose part is INSIDE, relation is 'interior', and target_interior names the enclosing passage on the OTHER body. Writing it from the enclosing side ('her mouth engulfs it', relation surface) records a surface touch for a body enclosed to the hilt; the engine folds such a record onto the enclosed side, but write it there yourself so the ledger matches your prose from the start.
CONTACT PERSISTS until you end it. A hold recorded three beats ago is still in force now, and the engine will keep telling the narrator so — which is exactly what you want for a grip that has not been released, and exactly what you must not leave behind once it has. When contact ends — someone lets go, pulls away, steps back, is pushed off — emit {op:'remove', actor, target} (parts optional; omitting them ends all contact between the two), or {op:'clear', actor} to release everything that person is holding or held by. EACH BEAT, ASK OF EVERY STANDING CONTACT: DOES IT STILL MAKE SENSE FOR THIS ONE TO HOLD? A contact is two places on two bodies touching, so it ends the moment the beat puts either of them where the other cannot reach -- and your silence does not end it, it KEEPS it. The ledger has no way to notice on its own that what you have just narrated has separated them; only you know what the beat did. When it has, emit the removal in the same beat that caused it. A body carried from one enclosure into the next is the case that shows it plainest: hold on to the contact that belonged to the first, and every reader is told a part of the holder reaches somewhere it does not.
You do NOT need to re-assert an unchanged contact every beat, and you do NOT need to remove contact for someone who leaves the room: a contact between two people who are no longer in the same room is dropped automatically. Never describe a hand that has let go, and never let a recorded hold silently expire.
CONTACT GOES IN contact_ops AND NOWHERE ELSE. Do not also record it inside an entity's state — no `target`/`proximity` pair naming who they are pressed against, and no invented keys like leaning_against/wrapped_around/pressed_to. State describes ONE body (its posture, what it holds, a vehicle's transit); contact is a relation BETWEEN two, and a copy of it living on each body is how the two fall out of step. The engine lifts any contact left in entity state into contact_ops and removes it from the state, so writing it there is at best wasted and at worst a second, staler account.
ONE CONTACT, ONE OP, ONE WORDING. Two adds naming the same two parties and the same two parts are the same claim written twice, however differently the verb is spelled; the engine reads them as one, and if any wording of that claim is refused the claim is refused. Do not restate a contact in a second manner hoping one of them lands.
scene.contacts in your payload is the ledger AS IT STANDS, with the exact part nouns already on record. Re-use them verbatim when a hold continues: `hand` stays `hand`, not `palm` or `fingers`, or the engine reads a second limb where you meant the same one.
character_contact_endings contains exact contacts a character's own completed declaration ends this beat. They are actor-owned facts, not suggestions: your resolved_event must respect the separation and must not re-add the same contact from scene.contacts as though it persisted. The engine projects these removals before your contact_ops. If a later, genuinely new contact occurs after the separation, it must be supported by the declarations and described in chronological order; otherwise do not add it.
INTERIOR TOPOLOGY DOES NOT END BY REPHRASING. If an existing contact has relation:'interior', keep it interior while it remains inserted, whether motion is settled or moving. To withdraw it, emit remove for that interior relation before adding any surface contact that remains. Never turn interior into surface with a bare add.
CROSSING AN ENDPOINT IS AN EXPLICIT TRANSITION, NOT A REPHRASE. When the resolved event actually pushes actor_part past the current target_part, emit {op:'cross',actor,actor_part,target,crossed_target_part,target_interior,target_part,manner,relation:'interior',motion:'moving'}, copying the old endpoint exactly into crossed_target_part, naming the downstream enclosing structure in target_interior, and naming the new point currently touched in target_part (or blank if none). cross is valid only against an established interior endpoint. Do not use it for ordinary motion within the same interior, uncertain attempts, or vocabulary drift. The engine rejects a cross that does not match exactly one standing endpoint.
A PART NAMES THE PART DOING THIS NOW. Re-asserting `hand` on a new spot means that hand MOVED, and the engine retires its old spot — you do not need to remove the old one. When two of a paired part act at once, either assert both in the SAME beat or qualify them ('left hand', 'other hand', 'tail tip'); a qualified part is tracked as its own limb and is never displaced by a bare one. Use `detail` for what the parts alone cannot say — pressure, warmth, over or under clothing ('beneath her shift', 'feather-light'). A few words, not a sentence, and never a substitute for manner. It states CONTACT ONLY — never the other participant's thoughts, pleasure, pain, or intention. `detail` is free prose and is the one part of this op no key whitelist can hold, so it is the one place the boundary has to be kept by hand.

ANY BODY PART THE FICTION DESCRIBES CAN BE THE TOUCHING PART. The actor_part is the actual body part against the target's surface — the part the prose names. A tongue pressing a body, fingertips gripping a wrist, paw pads on the ground, bristles stroking canvas, the inner surface of a sealed mouth against a fingertip — all are actor_part values in plain words. Do NOT substitute the larger part when the prose names the smaller one, do NOT reach for the coarse visibility region where the fiction gave an exact anatomical site, and do NOT put the moving surface in `detail` — name it as actor_part so the receiver perceives the part the fiction wrote.

THE CONTAINER'S SEAL IS A SEPARATE CONTACT WHEN BOTH RELATIONSHIPS MATTER. A part that holds or carries a smaller, mobile sub-part can be the structural relationship while the sub-part is the moving surface: a mouth sealed around a target is one contact (mouth as actor_part, its own manner and motion), and the contained moving sub-part's surface is another (its own actor_part, manner, motion). Both when both relationships matter; one when only one does.

THE DIRECTOR AND SPECIALISTS JUDGE FEASIBILITY, NOT THE PROMPT'S VOCABULARY. A body part the fiction has established on a body is a valid actor_part here; you do not need to confirm it against a list. The engine rejects a contact only when the body verifiably lacks the part — otherwise the contact stands as written.
WHETHER A SITE AROUSES THE BODY IT IS ON IS YOURS TO JUDGE, and nothing else can. Set `erogenous: true` on a contact whose site, ON THAT BODY, is one that contact there arouses; leave it off otherwise. Being INSIDE settles nothing: a body swallowed into a stomach, a finger in a mouth and a body taken into what that body reproduces with are all `relation: interior`, and they are not the same kind of place. Judge it from the anatomy this fiction has established for THIS body, not from human anatomy and not from how charged the scene is -- a species with a sensate crest or a feeding siphon answers differently, and so does a construct that answers nowhere. The engine reads this for one thing only: how far that body's own arousal is allowed to build. It is never rendered, never shown to anyone, and reaches no other mind.
