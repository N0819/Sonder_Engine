BEING CARRIED — CONTAINMENT: when a body stops being independently placed and is carried instead — picked up and held, put in a pocket or a jar or a bag, riding on a shoulder, mounted on a steed — record it in state_diff.containment as {subject: {in: <holder>, mode: 'held'|'carried'|'pocket'|'container'|'inside'|'riding'|'mounted'|'worn'}}. The mode decides whether they can still be SEEN: 'held', 'carried', 'riding', 'mounted' and 'worn' are carried in view; 'pocket', 'container' and 'inside' are shut away, and a body shut away is not visible to the room AND cannot see the room — it perceives by touch and by what it can hear. Anything you write that is not one of the five in-view modes is read as shut away, so use an in-view mode when they can still be seen. Release it with {subject: null} when they get out, are set down, are tipped out, or climb free. This is NOT the same as contact: a hand on a shoulder is contact, being IN the pocket is containment, and a carried body usually has both.
A THING IS OUT OF SIGHT BECAUSE SOMETHING IS BETWEEN IT AND THE ROOM, AND THAT SOMETHING HAS A NAME. This ledger holds objects on exactly the same terms as bodies. When a beat puts a thing where the room can no longer see it, write {thing: {in: <what is hiding it>, mode: <a shut-away mode>}}, naming a holder the scene already places — the board it went under, the chest it was shut into, the coat that pocketed it are illustrations of ONE record, not three cases. Everything subtracts from that record for free: while it stands the thing is not seen, not smelled, not reached. And it is what makes FINDING the thing an act with a result — release it ({thing: null}) on the beat something opens, lifts or moves what was hiding it, and the thing lies in the open where its holder stands. A word inside the thing's own state saying it is hidden is read by nothing, so nothing can ever say it was found.
A CARRIED BODY DOES NOT HAVE ITS OWN POSITION. The engine puts them wherever their carrier is, following the chain (in a jar, in a satchel, on a person). So do NOT move a carried body by writing a position — that will simply be overwritten. If they get out, release the containment first; then they can move on their own. And do not let a carried character act as though they were standing in the room: they cannot walk to the door, take something from a shelf, or step between two people while they are in a pocket. What they CAN do is act on their container and on whatever is within reach of it — squirm, shout to be heard, grab the lip of the pocket, be seen if they get out far enough to be visible.
Containment is released automatically when either body changes size, for the same reason a grip is: someone restored to full height is not still in a coat pocket. Re-declare it in the same beat if it still holds.
Shape: containment:{name:{in,mode}|null}.
