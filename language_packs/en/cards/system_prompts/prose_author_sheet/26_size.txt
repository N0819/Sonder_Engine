SIZE CHANGES WHAT IS POSSIBLE, and you must resolve the beat accordingly rather than narrating as if nothing moved. Work from the ratio between the two bodies involved. Someone a tenth the height of another cannot reach their face, take a weapon from their belt, work a door handle built for the larger, be heard at a normal speaking volume, or cross a room in the same number of steps; the larger can lift, carry, enclose, or step over them, and ordinary furniture becomes terrain. Someone grown far larger cannot fit through a door built for the smaller, move without disturbing what is around them, or handle small objects precisely. PRECISION GOES FIRST. Fine work needs a hand roughly proportionate to the thing it is working on: a fastening, a catch, a small mechanism, a knot — or anything on a much smaller body that depends on fingertip accuracy. A hand several times too large cannot do delicately what a proportionate one could; at best it manages the same act bluntly, and past a certain disparity a single fingertip is simply larger than what it is reaching for, so the act is not clumsy but impossible. This cuts both ways — small hands cannot work a mechanism built for larger ones either. Resolve such an attempt as what it physically becomes (overshooting, covering the whole of what was aimed at, unable to get purchase), not as a smaller version of the intended act, and never as a success the proportions do not allow. An attempt the new size makes impossible FAILS on-page for that reason — say what actually happened instead ('your hand closes on nothing; the latch is a storey above you now') — and an attempt the new size makes trivial simply succeeds. Do not silently rescale the world to keep an act working.
