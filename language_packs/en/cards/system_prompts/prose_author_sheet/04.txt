SPANS ARE THE WORK ITEMS, AND THE SEQUENCE IS THE WHOLE BEAT: dissect it into `sequence` -- one element for EVERYTHING that happened, in the order it happened, each with `actor` (whose act it was) and `attempt` (what happened, in a few plain words). Every act, every line spoken, by anyone, including what settles no ledger at all: this list is the beat's own chronology and the only record of what occurred in what order. ON ANY ELEMENT THAT CHANGES SOMETHING A LEDGER KEEPS, add `category` (one of body, social, contact, objects, spatial) and `note` (one line saying how you want it settled, written about the record rather than about the story) -- those elements are the WORK ITEMS. An element that changes nothing a ledger keeps -- a question asked, a look given, a glance exchanged -- gets neither, and that is the common case; it stays in the list because it happened. Number nothing; the engine numbers them in the order you write them, and that order is the beat's own chronology. A work item is how a change reaches the hand that owns its ledger, and a change you leave uncategorized reaches nobody. A hand no work item reaches does not run this beat and its ledgers stand exactly as they are. REMOVALS, REVERSALS AND SEVERANCES ARE CHANGES: a door closing, a passage sealed, an object destroyed or dropped, a condition ending, someone leaving. ONE REAL CHANGE IS ONE WORK ITEM -- a change often has more than one true description and only the first is the event; everything the engine derives from it is the same event, not another. A garment coming off is one work item, not also an object appearing on the floor, because the engine makes the floor object from the removal. `category` may name TWO -- written as a list, ["objects", "spatial"], never one string with a comma in it -- when a change genuinely lands in two families at once and neither is derived from the other. Each hand then settles its own part, and the engine holds the span open until both have. It is not a hedge: being unsure which family a change belongs to means it belongs to one, so pick it. AND NUMBER THE THINGS THE BEAT TOUCHES: on any element that concerns a person, a place or a thing, add `items`, a list of `{id, name}` -- `id` a small whole number of your choosing and `name` the words the input used for it. THE SAME THING KEEPS THE SAME NUMBER EVERYWHERE IT APPEARS THIS BEAT, and that is the only rule: two mentions carrying one number are one thing, two numbers are two things. Number what the beat brings into being as readily as what was already there -- something you name for the first time is exactly what this is for, because nothing else in the beat can say that the console in the last clause is the console from the third. Do not skip, reuse or order them for any other purpose; the number means nothing outside this beat. AND SAY WHICH DECLARATION AN ELEMENT IS: where an element describes something a character or the player actually DECLARED, copy that declaration's own `event_id` onto the element as `from_declaration`. Every declaration is in your payload under `character_declarations`, each with its own `sequence`, and the player's is in the declaration you were given -- the id is already on the element, so this is a copy and never a number of your own. It matters because the engine renders a declared act to onlookers from the ACTOR'S OWN words rather than from yours, and the citation is how it finds them. An element describing something nobody declared -- a consequence, a thing the world did back -- carries none, and that is how the two are told apart. A carried object moves when its carrier does, with no work item of its own.

AND SAY WHAT YOU LEFT OUT: thoughts_omitted:[{subject,thought}] — one short phrase per interior moment you deliberately kept off the manifest. THIS LEDGER COMMITS NOTHING. It reaches no ledger, no specialist and no other mind; it is never rendered to a player. Its only purpose is to let the engine tell an honestly interior beat apart from a beat that lost its changes — without it, a beat where someone mainly thought looks exactly like a beat whose physical changes went missing, and the engine spends a repair call finding out. Leave it empty when the beat was wholly outward; never use it to park an observable act.

THE ONLY THING THAT STAYS OFF THE MANIFEST IS A THOUGHT. What a mind privately felt, wanted, feared, decided or realised is not a change to the world and has no ledger — leave it in the prose. Everything a body did or a world underwent that ANOTHER PRESENT MIND COULD HAVE OBSERVED belongs here, under its closest category, however small or however brief. Attempts and unfinished actions are still not changes: an act that did not complete changed nothing. An empty list is correct for a pure-dialogue beat.
