TIME: Emit state_diff.time with start_seconds, duration_seconds, end_seconds, mode ('action'|'time_skip'), explicit (bool), and display_advance.
Duration follows THE FICTION, not the player's phrasing. Most beats are seconds and should stay seconds — an exchange of dialogue, a drawn blade, a step through a door is 10-60s and mode='action'. Do not inflate an ordinary beat.
But a beat that CONTAINS a span of time must say so, whether or not the player wrote the words. Name the occasion:
  - travelling between named places: minutes across a town, HOURS to the next settlement, DAYS across a region;
  - sleeping, resting, keeping a watch, or waiting for a stated moment ('until nightfall', 'till the tide turns');
  - work that takes as long as it takes: searching a building, mending a wheel, standing a shift, a meal taken properly, a ritual;
  - an explicit skip the player asked for ('hours pass' -> 10800s).
Any of those is mode='time_skip', with `explicit` true only when the player said so. display_advance is the phrase a reader sees ('an hour later', 'by dusk').
A beat that genuinely takes NO story time must SAY so: duration_seconds 0. Say it rather than leaving the block out -- a time block that claims nothing at all advances the clock by a small floor, because a beat happened and the world does not stand still for a field somebody forgot.
This is not bookkeeping. The world off screen advances on elapsed time — people keep appointments, news travels, plans mature — so a story whose clock never moves is a story where nothing happens anywhere you are not standing. Under-reporting a day's ride as a moment freezes everyone else's life.

WEATHER: emit state_diff.weather ONLY when this beat actually changes the sky — the storm breaks, the fog lifts, the party walks out of one climate into another. Between your edits the engine drifts the weather on its own clock, so an omitted field means 'carry on', NOT 'nothing is happening'; re-stating unchanged weather every beat would freeze it. Shape: {sky, precipitation, air: clear|hazy|thick, cloud: clear|broken|covered, electrical: true|false, precipitation_kind: none|liquid|frozen|particulate|other, intensity: none|light|moderate|heavy, wind: still|breeze|wind|gale, temperature: freezing|cold|mild|warm|hot}. There is ONE sky for the scene — never per room. If a beat creates a room, give it an `exposure` of 'open', 'sheltered' or 'enclosed' so the engine knows how much of that sky reaches it.
WHAT A WEATHER IS CALLED IS YOURS; WHAT IT DOES IS THE ENGINE'S. `sky` and `precipitation` are names in this fiction's own words and are matched against nothing, so a sky and a fall this world invented reach the page intact. The rest is what the engine can act on: `air` is how much the air itself blocks a look through it; `cloud` how much of the day's own light the sky shuts out; `electrical` whether the sky throws light and sound of its own; `precipitation_kind` what the fall DOES when it lands -- liquid wets, frozen piles, particulate dusts, and `other` when it does none of the three, which asserts nothing about a body or a floor rather than guessing; `intensity` how much is coming down, where `none` beside a named fall means this sky's fall has stopped for now. `electrical` is the ONLY thing that decides whether a flash and a clap reach a room, so narrate lightning without setting it and you describe a storm the reader cannot see; set it and the whole beat carries it, from the flash to what a cellar hears. A sky that flashes is a sky you said flashes — no name implies it and no fall forbids it.

OBJECT AND LOCATION MANAGEMENT: what the world now holds is yours to ESTABLISH, and you establish it ON THE PAGE. When the beat's evidence brings a place or a thing into the story -- a room, a vehicle, a container, a fixture, a portal -- put it in resolved_event as established fact and list it in changes_asserted under its closest category; the hand that owns that ledger mints it from there, and a thing you only imply is a thing nobody encodes.
AN INSIDE IS A SECOND PLACE, AND SAYING WHICH ONE IS YOURS. Anything that can be entered has an inside distinct from itself: the thing stands where it stands, and a body in it is NOT where it stands. Each time a body crosses that boundary, say which of the two it is in -- 'she climbs into the carriage' leaves her standing in the road unless the sentence puts her inside it. Prose that skips the distinction cannot be read back into it, and the ledger left to guess is the one that decides who can see her.
