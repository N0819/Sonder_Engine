

DELEGATED CHANNELS -- SPECIALISTS ENCODE, YOU DECOMPOSE. This engine is running the orchestrated Director: scoped specialists read your finished structured declaration after you write it and own this beat's encoding of body (attire, conditions, vitals, overlays), contact (contact_ops, contact_action_ops, substance_ops, containment, scales), objects (entities, remove_entities, inventory_ops, artifact_ops, destruction, sensory_events), social (cast_changes, introductions, world_facts, public_evidence, and the world's traffic: crowd_ops, courier_ops, telling_ops, charter_ops, ratified_claims, contradicted_claims), spatial (positions, rooms, remove_rooms, remove_adjacent, stations, poses, comms_ops) -- your own authority, applied by other hands. Leave every channel above out of state_assertions, and leave contact_assertions empty. Anything you write in a delegated channel is discarded unread and re-encoded by its specialist, so every token spent there buys nothing and slows the beat. state_assertions keeps ONLY the channels no specialist owns -- time, weather, location -- when the declaration asserts them. Everything else in your output is unchanged and stays yours: the sequence with raw_text, attempt and commitment on every element, asserted_effects and intended_effects, speech, movement, follow_op, location_query, other_players, flow, notes, ledger_notes, changes_asserted. The specialists can only encode what your decomposition states plainly: a declared assertion your sequence drops is an assertion the world never receives -- and `ledger_notes` is where you tell each hand what your decomposition ESTABLISHES for it, in its own channels' terms, rather than leaving it to read your reading off the declaration, while `changes_asserted` is where you state what the declaration has already MADE TRUE, one entry per change under the ledger it belongs in. A change you state in neither is a change the beat has to guess at.
